## The five most consequential verdicts

1. **ADOPT B: the editor stays a wizard, made free.** B keeps the four steps, but every step can be visited in any
   order, drafts save themselves, Edit opens the step you came from, and `@wide` gets a status outline column. That
   gets nearly all of my one-page editor's value at a fraction of the cost, and it keeps the wireframe. I withdraw
   A5. (Point 1.)
2. **ADOPT B: edits to a published recipe save to the device until "Save changes".** I missed that every server
   save makes a version. My "autosave every 10 s" would have written a version every 10 s on a published recipe.
   (Point 2.)
3. **MERGE: one-line ingredient entry with B's interaction and a different mechanism.** The cook types the whole
   line ("2–3 leeks, sliced"), sees a live reading, picks the food, and Enter commits. But the reader cannot come from
   `recipe-import-core`, because a guarded test keeps that package out of the Expo bundle. (Point 50.)
4. **KEEP A: Profile is the avatar, not a fourth tab.** The final structure must hold Plan and Shop. With Profile as
   a tab, that is six destinations, over the five a tab bar holds. The bar would then have to change after users
   learn it, and HIG names an unstable tab bar as the harm. (Point 39.)
5. **KEEP A: no standalone paste page.** "Paste ingredients" opens the editor with the paste sheet open, and the
   lines land as rows. B keeps a separate paste screen and review screen and adds "Start a recipe with these". That is
   two surfaces for one job, and the review screen repeats the ingredient list the editor already shows. (Point 40.)
