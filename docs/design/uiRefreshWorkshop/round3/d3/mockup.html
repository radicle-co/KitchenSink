Mise en Place, Set

A recipe with no photo gets a plate: the foods that make it what it is, set out on a board of the opposite colour, the way a commis sets a station before service. The plate never draws the finished dish. The recipe page takes the plate’s key for its title, so every recipe reads like its own page in a printed book. Everything you press stays basil. At night each board glows under a lamp of its own colour.

Layouts, flows and copy are the app’s own boards. Only the visual language changes. Every recipe plate on this page was resolved and laid out by the rule in the generator, with no hand fixes.

Lemon and thyme roast chicken

A Sunday roast that does the work for you: butter, lemon and thyme worked under the skin, and everything else cooking in the same tin so the vegetables catch the juices. Crisp skin, soft potatoes, and a pan sauce that needs no extra effort.

Ingredients

for 4 4 Edit
Chicken
  • 1 whole chickenabout 1.8 kg
  • 3 tbsp unsalted buttersoftened
  • 1 lemonzested and halved
  • 6 sprigs fresh thyme
  • 1 head garlichalved crosswise
  • 1 tsp flaky sea salt
Vegetables
  • 800 g waxy potatoesquartered
  • 3 carrotscut into chunks
  • 2 red onionscut into wedges
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Freshly ground black pepperto taste

Steps

Edit
  1. 1

    Heat the oven to 220°C (200°C fan). Take the chicken out of the fridge, pat the skin dry with paper towels, and leave it at room temperature while the oven heats.

  2. 2

    Mash the butter with the lemon zest, half of the thyme leaves and the salt. Work your fingers under the skin of the breast and spread the butter over the meat.

  3. 3

    Scatter the potatoes, carrots and onions in a large roasting tin and toss with the olive oil. Put the chicken on top with the halved lemon, the garlic and the rest of the thyme in the cavity.

  4. 4

    Roast for 20 minutes, then lower the oven to 190°C (170°C fan) and roast for another 1 hour 10 minutes, until the juices run clear.

    1 h 30 min
  5. 5

    Rest the chicken, loosely covered with foil, for 15 minutes before carving. Spoon the pan juices over the vegetables.

    15 min

The rule, on the flagship plate

The hero is chosen by a ladder, not by line order: a food the title names, then produce, citrus and spice, then herbs, then proteins and the main carb. Staples and the aromatic base are only supports, unless the title names them (French onion soup). The ground is the hero’s complement, the board a food stylist would choose. A support below 1.3:1 on that ground is skipped.

Two contrast checks, run by the generator

Light: a hero below 1.5:1 sits in a glazed or a ceramic bowl, whichever gives it more contrast, and the bowl itself must clear 1.3:1 on the ground. Dark: it keeps its place and gets a warm rim from the lamp.

The plate’s life

The seed is stored at first save. The foods on the plate are frozen only when the recipe is first published, so a draft never keeps a face it has outgrown.

1 · First saveOnly a title. The seed is stored: layout, mirror, accent. The ground and tool here are live and temporary.
2 · Still a draftTwo lines matched, so the chicken leads for now. A draft’s plate follows its lines quietly, with no motion. Freezing here would have kept the wrong face.
3 · Frozen at first publishOr when an import finishes. The assembly plays once, the next time the recipe page opens, never in the editor.
4 · Only if a drawn food leavesThe cook swaps the chicken for tofu, which has no motif yet. The chicken leaves; the next candidate (potato) takes its slot in a 400 ms cross-fade. Nothing else moves a frozen plate.
