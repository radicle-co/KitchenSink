Enamelware

The recipe you are cooking sits on one glossy enamel plate, and its rim counts your steps, one segment per step. Everything else is a calm, ordinary app with soft enamel edges, where cobalt means only what you press, and the steps you have set by pressing.

Home and My recipes

390 px, light. One glossy cobalt button per screen, New recipe, in its own band above the tabs. What is chosen (All, My recipes, list view) is filled in ink, so cobalt stays for what you press and the steps you have set. A recipe’s thumbnail is a dish: the cook’s photo on a plate, or with no photo an empty glazed dish with a rim and a well. There are no letters in circles, so recipes never look like people.

Recipe detail, by day and by night

The plate’s rim has one segment per step, with gaps, like page dots bent into a ring, so it reads as “where you are”, not “loading”. The words in its gap say it too. Tap a step’s number to set your place. With Screen on, once the plate scrolls away a small pinned plate keeps your place in view. The night frame scrolls: try it.

The rim, in every state

Discrete segments with gaps, never a smooth sweep, so it cannot be mistaken for a spinner. Done and current steps are cobalt; steps to come are a thin edge line. The words are always there, so you never have to read the ring.

By night. Steps to come are Edge on the dark plate, 3.66 : 1.

No photo? An empty glazed dish

A recipe with no photo never shows a made-up dish or a letter. It gets an empty enamel dish in one of six glazes, picked from the recipe’s id: a rim, a sunken well and one highlight. Glazes are decoration and never carry meaning.

By day: Delft, Celadon, Paprika, Mustard, Heather, Lagoon.

By night: deep glazes, so the dishes do not glow.

The palette: Enamelware

Each colour has one job. Ratios are WCAG 2.2 contrast, computed from the hex values by the same script that wrote this page’s colours; Lc is APCA 0.0.98G. Every text pair passes 4.5 : 1, every control edge or state mark passes 3 : 1, and all muted text is Lc 75 or more, in both modes.

Type

Nunito for titles: rounded ends, like an edge dipped in enamel. Andika for reading: SIL’s literacy face, with I, l and 1 all different and a plain, narrow zero, so “800 g” and “220°C” never look like code. Its digits are all one width, so amounts line up.
