Carton

Each recipe is packed like good groceries: its name is printed big on a sky-blue carton, and you see the food through a window cut below it. Around that one carton is the plainest app you know, so the food and the one red button are the only loud things on the screen.

Option 1 · Designer 1 · round 1 · palette “Carton”: Sky carton, Tomato action, white Shelf, Squid-ink band. The drawn dishes stand in for the cook’s own photos.

Light

One carton per screen, holding the thing the screen is for. Everything else is a white shelf: plain rows, three tabs and one Tomato button.

Dark

Dark follows the phone’s setting. The carton deepens to night-sky blue and its band stays reversed, light on dark; Tomato lightens so it still reads as the one thing to press.

Cooking, first run and other states

Cook mode has no carton, no glass and no photo: one step, big, with Next and Previous fixed at the bottom. The other states use the same parts with real copy.

Palette “Carton”

A white shelf, one sky-blue carton with a squid-ink band, and one Tomato action. Blue sits opposite the browns, golds and reds of cooked food, so the dish in the window reads warmer. Tomato is the only colour you can press. Contrast below is computed in this page from the same tokens the phones use.

Colour distance (CIEDE2000, script output): Tomato is 45–68 from every earlier-round action. Inside the palette, Tomato, Sky carton, Mustard and Radicchio are at least 20 apart in light and 25 apart in dark.

Light

WCAG ratio and APCA Lc on the Shelf. Text on the carton is Ink only; the band is Shelf on Squid ink.

Dark

The same roles at night. The band is always reversed: Shelf text on Squid ink.

Type

One family, Bricolage Grotesque, used two ways: condensed and heavy only for the name printed on the carton, open and plain for everything else.

Principles applied

  • Fresh in the look, familiar in the structureThree tabs, large titles, rows with chevrons, a stepper, chips and a docked button sit where people expect them (Jakob). The carton is the only new thing, and it is a picture, not a control (canon §2.1, §2.2 rules 1–3).
  • Food first, text never on the photoThe name is printed on colour above the window: no scrim, no tint. At large text the window moves to the top, so the dish stays on the first screen (§3.4 rule 4, §1.4).
  • A short choice, not a heroThe carton is 329 px tall, so all three dinners show above the tab bar at 390 px. Each has its own reason (§4, Hick).
  • One loud colour means pressTomato fills one button per screen and marks ticks, links and the current tab’s icon. Sky blue only ever holds pictures and type (§3.4 rule 1, von Restorff).
  • Vertical to read, sideways only to sampleIngredients, steps, nutrition and the library run down. Filter chips are the one sideways row, with the next chip peeking (§1.3).
  • Reasons from facts the recipe holdsTotal time with meal type, servings, difficulty and dietary flags are on every recipe. Nothing depends on a history we do not store (§4, data audit).
  • Two modes, two layoutsPlanning gets colour and the carton. Cook mode is opaque and neutral, 28 px, with fixed Next and Previous low on the screen (§4).
  • CalmNothing moves at rest. Presses scale to 98% in 100 ms; a tick fills in 180 ms; all of it is off with Reduce Motion (§4.1).
