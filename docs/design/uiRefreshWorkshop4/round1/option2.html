Principles applied

Familiar structure, fresh look

Tab bar, large title, back, stepper, checkboxes, chips, one filled button. What is new is how food is shown: a wide slice with a price card in front. The card holds the title and facts, encodes nothing, and has nothing to tap on it (canon §2.1, §2.2 rules 1–2).

Scroll direction

Dinners, ingredients, steps and the library are vertical, because you read or compare them. The only row is the filter chips, which are optional and show the next chip peeking (§1.3).

Home helps you choose

Three dinners for three stored reasons: total time, ingredient count and servings, all stored on the recipe. "Ready by" is the stored total time added to the clock. No invented reasons, and nothing about features not built (§4).

One action, low and large

One filled Prosciutto button per screen, in thumb reach, with a word on it: Start cooking, Back to step 3, Add a recipe, New recipe (Hick, Fitts, von Restorff).

Food is the colour

The slices are the largest and most colourful things on every planning screen. The button is a deep cured red, darker than any dish, with a word on it. No tint, and no text on a raw photo (§3.1, §3.4 rule 4).

Large text keeps the button

The slice shortens as text grows, so at 200% and 320 px Start cooking stays on the first screen (§1.4).

Cooking needs nothing remembered

Quantities in the steps, durations as timers, the current step marked. Cook mode: one 28 px step, labelled Next and Previous low, screen on from your tap, no card or photo. Motion: a press and a tick only, off under Reduce Motion (§4, §4.1).
