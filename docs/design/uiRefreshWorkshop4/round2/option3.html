Commise visual refresh · Workshop 4 · Round 2 · Option 3 (final)

Food Edge

Each dish sits square against the edge of the screen, like the plate in a field guide, and its words face it across clear space. You choose dinner by looking at the food, then confirm by reading one line.

The palette is Stainless & Duck Yolk: the cool grey of a kitchen sink, so the food is the warmest thing you see, and the deep orange of a duck-egg yolk on the one thing to press.

Light

390 px phones. Three tabs, large titles, one yolk button per screen, always low. The recipe page is shown at full length.

Dark

Dark follows the OS setting. The same Home and recipe page, then Home in its other state: a cook in progress, read from the cooking session this device holds.

Two more states

Cook mode, where the signature is banned: one step, neutral and opaque, with labelled Previous and Next fixed low. And Home on the first day, with nothing of the cook's yet.

Palette: Stainless & Duck Yolk

The cool, brushed grey of a kitchen sink and the deep orange of a duck-egg yolk. The grey makes every dish the warmest thing on screen. Duck yolk marks only what you press: the one button, a ticked box, the current tab. In light, its darker tone, Burnt yolk, carries links and focus because a pale fill cannot carry text. Principles applied

One signature, in the look only

The square plate flush to the screen edge is each recipe's own photo. It encodes nothing: name, time and reason are words beside it. Banned from cook mode, controls, sheets and the ingredient and step lists (canon §2.2 rules 1–3).

Grouping by space

A plate and its words sit 14 px apart; pairs sit 28 px apart, and the ruled lists 12 px. No card or strip boxes a pick (§1.2 proximity).

Scroll direction

Picks, ingredients, steps and the library are vertical. The only row is the filter chips, with the next chip peeking (§1.3).

A short choice with stored reasons

Three dinners sorted by ready time: total time plus the clock. Each adds one stored fact: a collection, other cooks' rating of your public recipe, a dietary flag (§4).

One primary, low and labelled

One yolk button per screen, docked above the tabs and named for what it does: "Cook shakshuka". Edit sits in the bar; Delete sits in More, at the top (§1.1 Fitts, von Restorff; §4.1 reach).

Large text is real

Each phone is a container. At 200% text the plate stacks above its words, stays at the edge and keeps 200 px of food. Nothing is hidden (§2.2 rule 11, WCAG 1.4.4).
