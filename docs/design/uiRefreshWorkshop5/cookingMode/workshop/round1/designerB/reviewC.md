# Designer B reviews designer C, round 1: "Glance from across the kitchen" (heuristic, untested with cooks)

## Steal

- **The full-screen alarm in the app.** The screen turns to the inverse surface once, with the task at 40, a
  count-up and a 112 pt Stop. It is the only round 1 alarm a cook across the room sees, and it does not flash (CK-030).
- **"Alarms are off" with Allow alarms.** It designs the state where the cook said no. Mine asks once and then goes
  quiet. I will copy it (CK-030).
- **"Next to do · in 8:12: Turn the potatoes".** It turns a wait into the next action plus a countdown (CK-033).
- **A doneness cue beside the clock.** The roast is out at 11:16, and the juices must run clear.
- **"+ Add a timer" and timers marked "yours"** (CK-031).

## What fails

- **The same action is shown twice.** The band says "Mash 3 tbsp butter with the lemon zest" at 40. The list says
  "Mash 3 tbsp butter with the zest" at 17. Two wordings of one fact drift apart (R5).
- **The stale far line,** which C names. A cook who never ticks sees action 1 for the whole step. A prompt that
  looks sure and is wrong is worse than no prompt (CK-008).
- **"Next ticks the rest" writes a false record.** Lines the cook skipped get marked as done. The record must stay
  true (CK-020).
- **The look-ahead is too thin.** "Then · Step 3" is one muted line. Cooks look ahead to find work for the gap, so
  they need what the next step needs, not only its name (CK-033).
- **The "11" button is a bare number.** R1 asks for words: "Ingredients for 4 · 11 items".
- **40 pt costs a lot.** At about 15 characters a line, the band fills the top third before any context. The
  research supports big text and also shows this trade (CK-016).

## Question

The far band goes stale. Why not show the step's goal there ("Butter the chicken"), and leave the actions to the
near list?
