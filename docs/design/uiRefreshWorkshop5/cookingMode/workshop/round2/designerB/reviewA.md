# Designer B reviews designer A, round 2 (heuristic judgement, untested with cooks)

## Steal

- **Before you start as five timed lines.** It is easier to read than my to-scale strip, and it says the same thing:
  where the work is and where the free time is (CK-007).
- **"Do it now" that keeps the place.** "Doing early · from step 5", then "Done · back to step 4", and step 5 later
  shows the job as done. This is the clearest answer in the round to "take work out of order" (CK-003).
- **The wait:** the countdown becomes the Now and leaves the dock (Fix 2), with one cue (CK-036). No cards.

## What still fails

- **The 38 pt line goes stale.** Done moves the focus one line at a time. A cook who works through three lines
  without a tap still sees line 1 at 38, and from across the room it reads as "do this now". A says the screen
  "claims only the place the cook last reported". The cook at a distance cannot know that (CK-008, Fix 3).
- **One tap per line taxes the experienced cook.** A asks this question too. A step with three lines needs three
  taps to move on. That is a step they must get past (brief §3b).
- **Clock times are back:** "At the table about 6:15", "Roast · out at 5:57". A roast that runs 20 min long makes
  the cook look late. The oven sets the pace, not the clock (CK-021).
- **Done names a number, not the move:** "Done · step 5 of 8". The cook has to turn a number into a task. Keep 2
  asked for the move ("Next: trim the beans"). The duplicate A worried about is avoidable: C puts the next step's
  name only in the button (CK-033).
- **The fast warning is an unconfirmed inference** (two timers, or two steps under 5 min). A wrong one teaches the
  cook to ignore it (CK-036).

## Question

A cook does all three lines of step 2 without a tap. At that moment, what does the 38 pt line say, and why is it true?

If round 3 converges on one design, its base should be C, because nothing on its screen can go stale. Carry A's place-keeping "Do it now" and its timed Before you start into it.
