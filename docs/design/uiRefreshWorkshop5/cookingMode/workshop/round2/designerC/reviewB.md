# Review of designer B, round 2 ("The shape of the whole cook"), by designer C

## Steal

- **All steps, with each running timer nested under the step that started it.** It is the best answer in the round to
  "which timer is which" (CK-031). On wide screens it gives the timers a home without a separate column.
- **"The roast is taking longer. That is normal: the oven sets the pace, and nothing here is late."** It is the right
  copy for a running-long state, and it answers my round 1 question (CK-021).
- **"Nothing needs you until the potatoes timer rings."** Words remove the duplicate number (Fix 2).
- **No clock times while cooking:** durations and a doneness cue only. And "Step 2 of 6 ⌄ All steps" in the title.

## What still fails

- **34 pt does not reach the bar you cite.** You give 14.5′ at 3 ft yourself, under the 16′ that SC-007's source
  implies. On the phone that is the cook's only device, the glance is short (CK-016).
- **Step titles need data nobody has.** "Butter the chicken", "Fill the tin" and "Oven on, chicken out" head every
  step and fill All steps. A dropped them for that reason. Imported recipes do not carry them (CK-034).
- **The step overflows with no cue.** On the phone step screen, "Put the lemon and 1 head garlic" is cut at the
  bottom. A cook at a metre does not know more is there (CK-008).
- **The fast warning is a sand card with checkboxes.** A tint, a frame and a second tick list in one block is the
  densest shape on the screen (Fix 1). A struck-through job stays in it too.
- **Strikethrough** on ticked lines and on "In already": A called it noise in my round 1, and it holds here.
- **During a wait, Done becomes an outlined "Read step 5".** A cook who is done early loses the usual forward move.

## Question

An imported recipe has six paragraph steps and no titles. What heads each step, and what does All steps list?

If round 3 converges on one design, its base should be A, because its step screen is the calmest. B's All steps view
and its running-long copy graft onto A cleanly as the second view.
