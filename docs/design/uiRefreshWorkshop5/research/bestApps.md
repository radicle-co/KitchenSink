# Best-designed mobile apps: an evidence-based reference set

Researched 2026-10-20. Purpose: give the owner a set of reference apps to judge design work against, based on what independent judges said rather than on taste. Nothing here is a design proposal.

## How to read this

- **Counting rule.** Agreement is counted by **source family**, not by award line. All Apple honours (Apple Design Awards (ADA), App Store Awards, Behind the Design, Newsroom) count as **one** family: Apple. MacStories reviews and its yearly MacStories Selects awards also count as one family. So an app that won an ADA and also an App of the Year has one family, not two.
- **Quotes.** Every quote below comes from a page I fetched, and each one has its URL. Some lines appeared only in search snippets, and I failed to open their pages. Those are marked **(unverified snippet)** and are not quoted as fact.
- **Quote wording.** Quotes keep the source's own words, so some quoted sentences are long.
- **Single-source** claims are marked **[single source]**.
- **What did not help.** Mobbin, Refero, Screenlane and screensdesign.com are screen libraries. Being listed there is not an endorsement. The recipe page on screensdesign.com makes this point itself. It says its examples "do not establish which treatment performs better" ([screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/articles/recipe-app-design-examples/)). The Verge refuses automated fetching, so I have no Verge evidence. Six Colors and Daring Fireball gave no usable design review of any app on the shortlist. One Daring Fireball post praising Halide was a paid sponsor note ("My thanks to Lux for sponsoring last week", [DF](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2020/12/06/halide-mark-ii)), so I excluded it.
- **Other award bodies.** Google Play, the Webbys, Red Dot and Fast Company did not overlap with this list for 2019 to 2026. They picked different apps:
    - Google Play's 2024 Best App was Partiful ([Google](https://blog.google/products/google-play/google-play-best-apps-games-2024/)).
    - The 2024 Webby for Best Visual Design (Function) went to Runway iOS ([Webby](https://winners.webbyawards.com/2024/apps-software/app-features/best-visual-design-function/304396/runway-ios)).
    - Fast Company's 2025 General Excellence went to the main NYT app ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/innovation-by-design/2025)).
    - Red Dot's 2022 award went to Vorwerk's Cookidoo recipe feature ([intive](https://intive.com/es/insights/intive-and-vorwerk-win-red-dot-design-award)).
- **Result.** Most of the evidence comes from two strong families, Apple and MacStories. A few smaller reviewers add to it.

## The apps that several judges rank highly

### 1. Halide (and Kino), by Lux Optics. Families: Apple, MacStories

- **Recognition.** ADA 2022 winner, Visuals and Graphics. Its sister app Kino was iPhone App of the Year 2024. MacStories reviewed Halide at length.
- **Apple's reasons (2022):** "focuses on the essentials… packs tons of features into a remarkably easy-to-navigate interface; its uncluttered, smartly organized UI mimics a film camera" ([ADA 2022](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2022/)).
- **MacStories (John Voorhees):** "The main UI is dominated by the camera's viewfinder." He also notes that "the UI contains so few obvious controls", that "The app makes abundant use of haptic feedback to let you know when certain operations have been completed", and calls it "an app that's a pleasure to use" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/halide-mark-ii-review-the-convenience-of-computational-photography-and-flexibility-of-raw-in-an-elegant-camera-app/)).
- **What makes it feel premium.** The content fills the screen and the controls recede. Tools stay hidden until you call them, and haptics confirm each action. A custom typeface is claimed by the developer (self-reported, unverified snippet).
- **Aging.** Mark II came out in 2020. Mark III shipped by early 2026 (unverified snippet, [Appleosophy](https://appleosophy.com/2026/01/29/former-apple-designer-and-halide-co-founder-returns-to-apple/)). The same team won App of the Year in 2024. So the approach still holds up. This is an inference.

### 2. Mela (recipes). Families: MacStories, Apple

- **Recognition.** MacStories Selects Best Design 2021, a full MacStories review, and an ADA 2025 finalist in Interaction.
- **MacStories Selects 2021:** "The app is striking." "Mela's design is just as beautiful and carefully tuned on the Mac and iPhone." On cooking mode: "The experience is reminiscent of a text editor's focus mode." "Each step is highlighted with other steps visible but dimmed." "The instructions are big and legible too." "It's the perfect design for standing in the kitchen at a distance from your iPad or Mac as you cook." Text is "spaced and styled for maximum readability" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/macstories-selects-2021-recognizing-the-best-apps-of-the-year/)).
- **MacStories review (Voorhees):** "The bright, yellow accent color and the clear, carefully laid out text are just part of the app's appeal." "I can't emphasize enough how much nicer Mela's clear, focused text is to read as you cook." "its design is far more refined than the typical 1.0 app." It "succeeds by virtue of its careful attention to the interface", like Reeder, the developer's earlier app ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/mela-an-elegant-and-innovative-recipe-and-cooking-app-for-iphone-ipad-and-mac/)).
- **Apple (2025):** "Like a perfectly prepared dish, this recipe app is all about getting the details right. When the app's sharp-looking cooking mode is activated, recipe steps, measurements, and timing details are subtly dimmed and highlighted at the proper time" ([ADA 2025](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/)).
- **What makes it feel premium.** One yellow accent. Large, well-spaced text. A three-column iPad layout that "takes full advantage of the screen without feeling cluttered". A cooking mode that dims everything except the current step. No source discussed its photography.
- **Aging.** It was praised in 2021 and again by Apple in 2025 for the same cooking-mode idea. That is the clearest evidence in this set that a recipe design still holds up.

### 3. Crouton (recipes). Families: Apple, MacStories, TapSmart

- **Recognition.** ADA 2024 winner, Interaction. Reviewed by MacStories and TapSmart.
- **Apple:** "Recipe apps rise and fall on their organization and information hierarchy, and Crouton serves up a clean, tasty interface… Everything in Crouton, from the next recipe step to the name of that missing spice, is easy to find and right where you need it. And its effortless series of interactions mean people can keep their focus on the counter rather than their screen" ([ADA 2024](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2024/)).
- **MacStories (Ryan Christoffel):** "Rounded rectangles are used to house each of the different blocks of data available for recipes." "On a recipe's detail screen, everything is neatly organized in a layout that makes sense." "Crouton's playful aesthetics help make recipes feel more approachable to me." "It's modern, keeps things simple in a good way, and offers valuable utility with style" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/crouton-review-an-elegant-modern-recipe-manager-and-cooking-aid/)).
- **TapSmart (2024):** "Crouton formats recipes beautifully" and is "one of the smartest recipe manager apps we've ever used" ([TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/apps/review-crouton/)).
- **What makes it feel premium.** Content is grouped into clear blocks, and the hierarchy is easy to scan. Cooking mode shows one step at a time and moves on with a swipe. It has a slightly playful tone. Dark mode is supported.
- **Aging.** One early review dates from July 2020 (unverified snippet, [Appleosophy](https://appleosophy.com/2020/07/19/app-review-%e2%80%8ecrouton/)). It won the ADA in 2024. If the 2020 date holds, the design lasted four years. This is an inference.

### 4. Flighty. Families: Apple, MacStories

- **Recognition.** ADA 2023 winner, Interaction. Reviewed by MacStories. The Sweet Setup also praised it ("serious design chops", unverified snippet, because the page returned 403).
- **Apple:** "a beautifully designed flight tracker… With key information where you need it most, an intuitive interface, comprehensive live maps, and a look that mirrors time-honored airport design conventions" ([ADA 2023](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2023/)).
- **MacStories (Voorhees):** "Simply by having a top-notch design", Flighty outperforms most of its rivals. It uses a "card-like UI that peeks out from the bottom of the iPhone's screen" and "simple, brightly-colored iconography". "Everything is easy to read" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/flighty-a-pro-level-ios-app-for-frequent-travelers/)).
- **Against it.** Hacker News users criticised small, low-contrast text (unverified snippet). MacStories said the blurred "Try Pro" placeholders clash with the design (search summary).
- **What makes it feel premium.** It borrows a real-world visual language, airport signage. Colour is used for status only. One sheet holds the detail instead of a stack of screens.

### 5. Things 3. Families: Apple (2017, before this window), MacStories

- **Recognition.** ADA 2017 and ADA 2009, outside the 2019 to 2026 window. Still widely cited as a model.
- **MacStories (Christoffel):** "The app is dominated by white space, but it uses bold fonts, lovely icons, and thoughtful splashes of color." "It's simple, but beautiful in its simplicity." "the pop of the interface forms a perfect marriage with haptic feedback" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/things-3-beauty-and-delight-in-a-task-manager/)).
- **Aging.** It has kept the same look since 2017 and is still recommended in 2026 roundups (low-quality snippet sources). It is the longest-lived calm, white-space design in this set. Its evidence inside the window is weak, so weigh it as an example of a design that ages well, not as a recent winner.

### 6. Pixelmator Photo. Families: Apple, MacStories

- **Recognition.** ADA 2019 winner and MacStories Best Design 2022.
- **MacStories:** "Photo editing apps are tricky to design, especially on the iPhone… Pixelmator Photo pulls it off exceptionally well" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/macstories-selects-2022-recognizing-the-best-apps-of-the-year/)).
- **Why it matters.** It fits dense tools onto a small screen without looking crowded.

### 7. (Not Boring) apps and CARROT Weather. Families: Apple, MacStories

Both have strong agreement but the wrong tone for this brief.

- **(Not Boring).** Habits won the ADA in 2022: "sensational designs, playful haptics, and elegant gamification" ([ADA 2022](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2022/)). Weather was an ADA finalist in 2021, and Camera was a 2026 finalist and MacStories' 2025 Best Design runner-up.
- **CARROT Weather.** ADA 2021 winner for "simplicity and elegance" ([ADA 2021](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2021/)), and MacStories' 2022 runner-up.
- **Why they are excluded.** Both are loud and playful, not calm. They show that judges reward finish and craft. Their style does not fit a calm brief.

### Apple-only, but recognised more than once (one family each)

- **Gentler Streak.** ADA finalist in 2023 and winner in 2024. Its makers were finalists again in 2026. Apple: "relies on a subtle and consistently pleasing design language… powered not by insistent reminders but an optimistic and encouraging vibe" ([ADA 2024](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2024/)). This is the closest any juror came to the word "calm" for a fitness app.
- **Headspace.** ADA finalist in 2022 and winner in 2023: "thoughtful design decisions, an incredible UI, and a recognizable, comfortable illustration style… remarkably easy to navigate" ([ADA 2023](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2023/)). Apple says it has set the standard for "more than a decade". That points to aging well.
- **Tiimo.** ADA finalist in 2024 and App of the Year 2025. Apple says it uses "soothing colors" and made to-dos "a calming activity".
- **Tide Guide.** ADA finalist in 2023 and winner in 2026: "integrates the new design and Liquid Glass, adding extra polish to data presentation and custom animations" ([ADA 2026](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/)). Note that in 2026 Apple's idea of "premium" partly means adopting Liquid Glass.
- **Lumy.** ADA finalist in 2025 and Watch App of the Year 2024: "a simple design, carefully curated color palette, and elegant widgets" ([ADA 2025](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/)).
- **Vocabulary (2025 finalist) [single source]:** "Typography and iconography feel modern and balanced… A lovely instance of an app that quietly does everything right" ([ADA 2025](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/)).
- **Loóna (2021 winner) [single source]:** "Every aspect of its interface — type, color, animation, and 3D content — has been crafted with precision to create a soothing, calming experience" ([ADA 2021](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2021/)).

### MacStories-only

- **Mercury Weather (Best Design 2023) [single source].** "designed to be both beautiful and delightfully simple". The write-up mentions gradients, strong typography and generous white space ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/macstories-selects-2023-recognizing-the-best-apps-of-the-year/)).
- **Sequel (Best Design 2025) [single source].** "admirable, considered, and self-assured" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/macstories-selects-2025-recognizing-the-best-apps-of-the-year/)).

## (2) Best recipe apps by the same test

1. **Crouton**: the most families. Apple's citation is about hierarchy, which is the core job of a recipe app.
2. **Mela**: nearly tied. It has the strongest design-specific praise, from two families across four years.
3. **Pestle**: a distant third, from a single source (MacStories).

## (3) Qualities these apps share

1. **One job, done with focus.** Halide "focuses on the essentials". Mela's cooking mode is "reminiscent of a text editor's focus mode".
2. **Content first, interface second.** Halide's "main UI is dominated by the camera's viewfinder" with "so few obvious controls".
3. **Clear hierarchy, so things are easy to find.** Crouton: "Recipe apps rise and fall on their organization and information hierarchy… everything… is easy to find and right where you need it."
4. **Large, readable type and generous white space.** Things is "dominated by white space" with "bold fonts". Mela's text is "spaced and styled for maximum readability". Pestle shows steps "in big text". Mercury Weather pairs strong typography with white space.
5. **One restrained accent colour with a meaning.** Mela's yellow, Pestle's green for measurements, Flighty's colour for status, Lumy's "carefully curated color palette", Tiimo's "soothing colors".
6. **Hide the rest until it is needed.** Halide's hidden tools, Flighty's card that "peeks out", Craft's panels "tucked out of the way".
7. **Designed for the real setting.** Mela and Crouton are built for reading at arm's length while cooking. The current step is lit and the others are dimmed. Crouton: "keep their focus on the counter rather than their screen."
8. **Quiet motion and haptics that confirm actions.** Halide's "abundant use of haptic feedback". In Things, "the pop of the interface forms a perfect marriage with haptic feedback". Apple praised Vocabulary's haptics as "a nice touch".
9. **A consistent, gentle visual language.** Gentler Streak's "subtle and consistently pleasing design language". Vocabulary "quietly does everything right".
10. **Native to each device.** Mela is "just as beautiful and carefully tuned on the Mac and iPhone". The winners use widgets, Live Activities and the Dynamic Island, and in 2026 Liquid Glass. The juries treat fit with the platform as part of feeling premium.

**Limits of this research.** Summaries came through a fetch tool, so short quotes match the fetched page, but I did not read every page in full. The Verge blocked fetching. The 2020–2022 App of the Year data is incomplete. No source rated photography or long-term aging directly. The aging judgements above are inferences from repeat recognition.
