# Personality versus restraint: when expressive UI reads as premium, and when it reads as cartoony

Research date: 22 October 2026. Desk research only. No users were observed.

## How to read the evidence

Each claim carries a label.

- **[Primary]**: the company's own words, its design team, its guidelines, or its filings.
- **[Company claim]**: a number the company reports about itself. Nobody checked it.
- **[Press]**: a trade or news writer, quoted.
- **[Independent study]**: research by a party with no stake in the brand.
- **[Untraced]**: I saw the claim but did not reach the source. Do not quote it.

The quotes below were checked against the raw text of the page, except where marked.

## The main finding first

The owner named Lemonade and Headspace as favourites. Both are **restrained systems with one loud thing in them**. They are not illustration-everywhere apps.

- Lemonade's co-founder described the launch brand as "white, hollow line art, big text, plain English, clear call to action". He credited the "glorious pink button" for a click-through rate of almost 30%. [Primary] Source: [Lemonade blog, "Lemonade's Launch Metrics Exposed"](https://www.lemonade.com/blog/lemonade-launch-metrics-exposed/)
- In its 2023 refresh, Headspace added photography and chose a typeface that can "flex from playful to clinical". The writer saw a more professional persona. [Press, quoting Headspace] Source: [It's Nice That, April 2024](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)

So the minimal redesign was probably rejected for what it lacked: a bold colour, motion and a voice. That is my reading of the evidence, not something the owner said. If it is right, what the redesign lacked was not drawings.

## The cases

### 1. Lemonade (insurance)

**What they did.** At launch in 2016 the brand was black and white line art with one hot pink. Buying insurance was a chat with a bot called Maya, one question at a time. Claims went to a bot called AI Jim.

**Rules.** In the co-founder's words, everything starts with "white, hollow line art, big text, plain English, clear call to action". The app is meant to "make buying insurance feel like a friendly conversation". Pink is kept for the one action. "The brand colors (or lack of) and the glorious pink button help users focus on a single, clear action to take." [Primary] Source: [Lemonade blog, Shai Wininger](https://www.lemonade.com/blog/lemonade-launch-metrics-exposed/)

The bot has a human anchor. "A.I. Jim is the digital incarnation of Jim Hageman, our Chief Claims Officer." The bot also says what it cannot do: "A.I. Jim is still learning, and he often escalates claims to real Jim." [Primary] Source: [Lemonade blog, "Lemonade Sets a New World Record"](https://www.lemonade.com/blog/lemonade-sets-new-world-record/)

**What people said.** "The conversation with Maya feels natural… neither tedious, nor do they feel like you are filling out an insurance form." Also: "Lemonade truly achieves its goal of making insurance instantaneous and delightful." That was Andy Vitale, a UX director. [Press] Source: [Adobe Blog, Dec 2017](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2017/12/14/uxperts-weigh-in-designs-we-love-december-edition-2)

**Results.** In the first 48 hours, home page click-through was "almost 30%" and conversion from quote to payment was "14.8%". [Company claim] The claim paid in 3 seconds was also self-reported. Guinness "told us that no international standards exist" for the record. [Company claim] Sources are the two Lemonade posts above.

**Aging.** The personality sat in the speed and the voice, not in drawings. The look is still recognisable ten years on. I found no critic who called it childish.

### 2. Headspace (meditation, then mental health care)

**What they did.** From 2011 the brand was built on simple characters and animation. When Ginger became Headspace Care in October 2023, Headspace refreshed the brand. Press covered it in April 2024. I found no 2021 rebrand.

**Rules.** The first head of design, Anna Charity, said the aim was to remove "the mysticism and hippy vibe". Characters had a job: "By using characters and storytelling, we could break down the barriers of a tough subject matter, and present it in a light-hearted but sensitive way." [Primary] Source: [Creative Lives in Progress](https://creativelivesinprogress.com/articles/anna-charity)

Illustrator Karen Hong: "it may look like Iʼm just drawing cute smiley faces and characters all day", but the work is to "communicate abstract ideas and tougher topics… Visual metaphors are key!" [Primary] Source: [Dribbble, 2020](https://dribbble.com/stories/2020/03/27/headspace-design-karen-hong)

The 2023 refresh changed the faces. They now show a "range of emotions beyond a smile... not just joy — but stress, sadness, contentment". Photography shows the clinical services. The palette leads with orange, and the writer says it now takes more care with contrast. [Press, quoting Headspace] Source: [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)

**What people said.** It's Nice That called the photography "a risk considering how impactful that lone animated floating head was". [Press] Same source.

**Results.** Over 100 million downloads, "a 60+ NPS", and "73% of the company's enterprise clients cite the Headspace brand as a key driver of employee adoption". [Company claim] Source: [Headspace, 31 Oct 2023](https://organizations.headspace.com/blog/headspace-unveils-refreshed-brand-and-expands-offerings-for-lifelong-mental-health-support)

**Aging.** The serious work got photography and a clinical register, while the characters stayed for emotion. Personality moved toward the edges as the stakes rose.

**[Untraced]** A secondary summary said a Headspace designer called the old style "childish and cartoony". I did not get the source to open (Blush, which refused both fetches) and no other source carries the quote. Do not cite it.

### 3. Duolingo (language learning)

**What they did.** The team first redrew Duo the owl in 2013. In January 2019 the in-house team simplified him again "to make it easier to animate" (Creative Bloq, below). Later that year Johnson Banks rebranded the company with a typeface drawn from Duo's feathers. In 2020 Duolingo added a cast of human characters. It now animates them in Rive.

**Rules.** The ground rules for the 2013 redraw: "Duo's color and his species… were not going to change". The silhouette came first: "if the overall shape is not appealing, then the details within that shape can't compliment it successfully." [Primary] Source: [Duolingo blog, "Reshaping Duo", Nov 2013](https://blog.duolingo.com/reshaping-duo/)

The cast had to look like Duo, using his "four main components" of geometric body, big eyes, unique shape and detached feet. The team rejected Vikings and aliens because they "felt too far removed from the reality of language learning". Characters appear where the learning happens, speaking the sentence and reacting to a correct answer. The work took "a year and a half". [Primary] Source: [Greg Hartman, Duolingo blog, Nov 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/building-character/)

Johnson Banks: "So many tech and Silicon Valley brands have adopted the same neutral, characterless sans-serif typography… We were determined to find something that stood out." [Press, quoting the agency] Source: [Creative Review, Sept 2019](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/duolingo-rebrand-johnson-banks/)

Animation is engineered, not decorated: "with interactivity, how it's built really matters." [Primary, Duolingo staff quoted by Rive] Source: [Rive blog](https://rive.app/blog/creative-technologists-duolingo-s-solution-to-the-designer-to-developer-handoff)

**What people said.** Creative Bloq said the 2019 interface got "a more cartoony refresh". It also said Duolingo had "mastered the art of emotional manipulation with Duo". [Press] Source: [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/duolingos-redesigned-mascot-is-a-hoot)

**Results.** Duolingo says social impressions grew 150% year on year (Q2 2023), and that "We've made our brand more iconic, especially our mascot Duo" (Q1 2024). In Q3 2025 it said growth slowed "in part because we posted less 'unhinged' content". [Primary, SEC filings] Sources: [Q2 2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208823000173/duolingo_q2-2023xshareho.htm), [Q1 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208824000110/q1fy24shareholderletter.htm), [Q3 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828025049514/q3fy25duolingo9-30x25share.htm)

**Aging.** It works because the product is a game and the stakes are low. It is a poor model for a task where a mistake costs money or health.

### 4. Airbnb (the Bélo era and the 2025 redesign)

**What they did.** In 2014 DesignStudio made the Bélo symbol. In May 2025 an in-house team rebuilt the app with soft, three-dimensional, animated icons.

**Reception of Bélo.** The symbol was mocked on launch. Erik Spiekermann said "someone didn't do their homework", and Gizmodo called it a "Sexual Rorschach Test". [Press] Source: [Dezeen, July 2014](https://www.dezeen.com/2014/07/17/new-airbnb-logo-comparisons-automotive-anywhere-habitat-genitalia/) Ten years later Creative Review said it has "a reassuring familiarity". [Press] Source: [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/the-airbnb-rebrand-10-years-on/) The early anger was about the shape, not about tone.

**2025 rules.** VP of design Teo Connor said of booking a home: "we needed to enhance that, not get in the way of it". On the icons: "They've got a ton of utility, they're a universal language, and they're playful." The target was "Fun, alive, and simple". The writer says the team built the icons slowly over two years. [Press, quoting Airbnb] Source: [It's Nice That, May 2025](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/airbnb-app-redesign-140525) Hiroki Asai said the system adds "little touches, surprises, and serendipity that make the whole thing feel intentional". [Press] Source: [Domus](https://www.domusweb.it/en/news/gallery/2025/05/16/airbnb-experience-app-design.html)

**What people said.** Kyle Chayka called the icons "softly rounded pictographs, like custom emoji". He warned that "a design entrepreneur on X already demonstrated how he used AI to replicate" them. [Press] Source: [One Thing newsletter](https://onethingnewsletter.substack.com/p/airbnbs-relaunch-and-the-texture) Design Compass reported "mixed responses from users". Brian Chesky followed with "Thank you for your feedback on our summer release design work" and a new design system. [Press] Source: [Design Compass, July 2025](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/07/04/airbnb-new-design-system/)

**Results.** None published.

**Aging.** It is too early to judge. Rich craft is easy to copy now, so it alone no longer signals care.

### 5. Bold colour in finance: Cash App, Monzo and Revolut

**Cash App.** BUCK rebuilt the 3D illustration system. Cash App ruled out becoming "another minimalistic brand lost in a sea of sameness". The work aimed to be "more mature, more focused, and lighter in touch". BUCK also avoided "an immediate dive into design, as that often leads to trendy work". Even for banking, security and scam protection, "preserving that unique identity was paramount". [Primary, agency case study] Source: [BUCK](https://www.buck.co/work/cash-app-visual-identity) The 2025 refresh made the green brighter, added the typeface Cash Sans and over 650 icons, and brought in motion and haptics. [Press] Source: [Design Compass, Dec 2025](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/12/03/cash-apps-new-design-language-and-ai-moneybot/)

**Monzo.** The tone rules fence humour. "Humour is a delicate seasoning… This belongs almost exclusively in our brand and marketing writing, with the occasional surprise in the app." On money worries: "We always handle those feelings with care and never make light of them." On craft: "Dropping the wand emoji in or talking about how magical things are just undermines that." [Primary] Source: [Monzo tone of voice](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice) Writer Harry Ashbridge said users like the emoji "but maybe you could stand to use a few less". [Primary] Source: [Nick Parker interview](https://nickparker.co.uk/the-bank-that-likes-to-smiley)

**Revolut.** Revolut 10 (2023) went the other way: a "minimalist interface" with "themes, widgets, and backgrounds" that the user chooses. [Press release text] Source: [Qorus](https://qorusglobal.com/content/19090-revolut-unveils-revolut-10-a-fresh-look-at-online-banking)

**[Untraced]** The story that Monzo's coral came from a designer's trainers. It comes from a blog post that did not open for me.

**Lesson.** In money, a loud colour is safe if the words and the flows stay calm and exact. Humour is kept away from moments of stress.

### 6. Mailchimp (Freddie and the 2018 Collins rebrand)

**What they did.** Collins simplified Freddie the chimp. It brought in Cavendish Yellow and Cooper Light, and illustrations from several artists. [Press] Source: [Creative Review, Sept 2018](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/mailchimp-goes-yellow-in-rebrand-by-collins/)

**Rules.** Freddie "smiles, winks, and sometimes high-fives, but he does not talk. Don't write in his voice." The voice rules: "We prefer the subtle over the noisy, the wry over the farcical", and "forced humor can be worse than none at all. If you're unsure, keep a straight face." [Primary] Source: [Mailchimp style guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) Head of brand Mark DiCristina: "we never try to be playful in moments where there might be some frustration." The famous "sweaty monkey finger" over the send button "acknowledged the anxiety". [Press, quoting Mailchimp] Source: [Creative Review, Jan 2020](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/mailchimp-on-playfulness/)

**What people said.** Creative Review noted Mailchimp "isn't the only tech company trying to soften its image using illustration". [Press] Source: Creative Review 2018, above.

**Results.** Forbes valued Mailchimp at "more than $4 billion" (reported by Creative Review). That is not a measure of the design.

**Aging.** It aged well because the character is small, silent and placed at real emotional moments.

### 7. Slack (2019 rebrand)

**What they did.** Slack's in-house team and Pentagram replaced an 11-colour hash mark that was "extremely easy to get wrong". Slack promised the new look would not "keep you from the important business of getting things done". [Primary] Source: [Slack blog](https://slack.com/blog/news/say-hello-new-logo)

**What people said.** "Crisp, clean, very well executed, and entirely lacking in soul." [Press] Source: [Web Designer Depot](https://www.webdesignerdepot.com/2019/01/slack-adopts-generic-new-identity/) A Design Week headline reads "Michael Bierut: 'Slack knew 95% of people would hate the change'". I saw only the headline. Source: [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/21-27-january-2019/michael-bierut-slack-knew-95-of-people-would-hate-the-change/)

**Lesson.** Restraint without a point of view reads as generic, which is the same failure as the owner's rejected minimal redesign.

### 8. Google Material 3 Expressive (2025)

This is the largest study I found, but it is Google's own research on its own product.

**What they did.** "46 separate research studies with hundreds of designs, and more than 18,000 participants". [Primary] Source: [Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)

**Findings, in Google's words** (same source):

- Preference: "strongly preferred by people of all ages, with that preference being particularly strong — up to 87% — among 18-to-24-year-olds."
- Speed: "Participants were able to spot key UI elements up to four times faster."
- Age: "a dramatic erasure of age effects in fixation times, helping 45-plus-year-old users perform on par with their younger counterparts."
- Desirability: "a 34% boost in modernity".
- Some people want calm: "a strong minority of users preferred calmer, less intense versions."
- Context: "What works in a media player or email app might not be suitable for something like a banking interface."
- Failures: album art arranged "helter-skelter" looked "modern and exciting" but "usability scores suffered". Also, "removing text labels from email actions resulted in decreased usability."
- The rule: "No amount of emotion can compensate for a lack of clarity."

Vanessa Cho, Google Design VP: "you might not want a super-playful UI for paying a parking ticket". [Press] Source: [Dezeen, May 2025](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/05/28/google-ushers-in-age-of-expressive-interfaces-with-material-design-update/)

**Important.** Google's "expressive" means size, colour, shape, containment and motion that point at the key action. It does not mean mascots or drawings.

**[Untraced]** Reports that users called the result "infantile" or "juvenile". The pages refused my fetches.

### 9. Apple: personality inside a restrained system

**Fitness rings.** Apple's guidelines protect one playful object with strict rules: "Don't use Activity rings for decoration… Never display Activity rings in labels or background graphics." "Never change the colors of the rings." "Always display Activity rings on a black background." [Primary] Source: [Apple HIG, Activity rings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings)

**Motion.** "Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion." "In apps, generally avoid adding motion to UI interactions that occur frequently." "Don't make people wait for an animation to complete." [Primary] Source: [Apple HIG, Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)

**Weather.** The iOS 15 backgrounds show the real sun, cloud and rain, so the delight is the data itself. Apple's "thousands of variations" figure reached me only through secondary coverage. Source: [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/2021/06/07/revamped-ios-15-weather-app-shows-off-a-whole-lot-of-dark-sky/)

**Memoji.** Memoji (2018) is the user's own face, inside Messages and FaceTime, and never in system chrome. I used secondary sources only. Source: [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2018/06/04/apple-reveals-memoji-ios-12)

### 10. Counter-examples

**Corporate Memphis ("Alegria").** Buck made the style for Facebook in 2017, and other brands copied it widely. Critics called it "lazy" ("simple shapes [and] untextured colours", Jack Hurley via Wired). [Press, via Wikipedia] Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_Memphis) Rachel Hawley explained the backlash: figures "meant to communicate inclusivity, cooperation, and equity while serving as the ebullient face for companies that profit off of social atomization". Another reason was that flat work is "cheap to produce". [Press] Source: [AIGA Eye on Design, March 2021](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/the-internet-is-turning-on-big-techs-colorful-corporate-mascots/) A defence: the style "has a rich, long history". [Press] Source: [AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/what-the-think-pieces-about-corporate-memphis-tell-us-about-the-state-of-illustration/) The style read as cheap because it was generic, easy to buy, and cheerful about things that were not cheerful.

**Cracker Barrel (August 2025), walked back.** The chain removed its "Old Timer" character for a plain wordmark. After a week of backlash it said: "Our new logo is going away and our 'Old Timer' will remain." [Press] Source: [NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/return-of-the-old-timer-cracker-barrel-scraps-new-logo-following-backlash/6377097/) The fight was partly political. This was not a case of cartoony design failing. It shows that taking out a loved character can also fail.

**The strongest independent evidence: NN/g, tone of voice.** A playful car insurer was rated friendlier but less trustworthy: "The friendliness kind of takes away some of the credibility." A casual bank was rated friendlier and more trustworthy than a formal one. Humour split people, with some calling it "corny". Trust explained "52% of the variability" in willingness to recommend, against 8% for friendliness. NN/g also warns: "If you're frequently hearing feedback from your stakeholders like, 'Make it exciting!' or 'Add a joke,' bear this example in mind." [Independent study] Source: [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/)

## 1. Principles that separate premium personality from cartoony

1. **Clarity first, then personality.** Expression must point at the task. Google found decoration that broke patterns hurt usability. "No amount of emotion can compensate for a lack of clarity." ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)); "enhance that, not get in the way of it" ([Airbnb via It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/airbnb-app-redesign-140525)).

2. **One loud colour on a quiet base.** Lemonade used black, white and pink, and kept pink for the main action ([Lemonade](https://www.lemonade.com/blog/lemonade-launch-metrics-exposed/)). The Activity rings always sit on black ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings)).

3. **Fence illustration to moments.** Use it for empty states, errors, onboarding and big wins, not as filler. "Excessive illustrations increase cognitive load," and decoration stays out of the product ([Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/illustrations)). "Don't use Activity rings for decoration" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings)).

4. **Match the tone to the stakes and the person's state.** Make no jokes at a moment of frustration or worry ([Mailchimp via Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/mailchimp-on-playfulness/); [Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice)). There is no super-playful UI for a parking ticket ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/05/28/google-ushers-in-age-of-expressive-interfaces-with-material-design-update/)). The playful insurer lost trust ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/)).

5. **Casual is not the same as jokey.** Plain, warm words raised trust at a bank. Chummy jokes lowered it at an insurer ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/)). Monzo's Ts&Cs are "readable by an 11-year-old" ([Nick Parker](https://nickparker.co.uk/the-bank-that-likes-to-smiley)).

6. **Use one character, or a strict family, never a crowd.** Duolingo built its cast from Duo's four parts over 18 months ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/building-character/)). Freddie is one silent mark that "does not talk" ([Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)).

7. **A character must do a job.** Headspace characters carry "tough subject matter" ([Anna Charity](https://creativelivesinprogress.com/articles/anna-charity)). Duolingo characters speak the exercise ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/building-character/)). Lemonade's bot is a real person's "digital incarnation" ([Lemonade](https://www.lemonade.com/blog/lemonade-sets-new-world-record/)).

8. **Craft and consistency are what make it premium.** The silhouette comes first ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/reshaping-duo/)). Icons were built slowly ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/airbnb-app-redesign-140525)). BUCK avoided "trendy work" ([BUCK](https://www.buck.co/work/cash-app-visual-identity)). Generic, easy-to-buy art reads as cheap ([AIGA](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/the-internet-is-turning-on-big-techs-colorful-corporate-mascots/)).

9. **Carry personality in motion, type and copy before drawings.** Google's gains came from size, colour, shape and motion ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). Duolingo put character into a typeface ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/duolingo-rebrand-johnson-banks/)). Lemonade put it into a conversation.

10. **Keep motion short, and rare on repeated actions.** Do not make people wait ([Apple HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)). Duolingo engineers its animation ([Rive](https://rive.app/blog/creative-technologists-duolingo-s-solution-to-the-designer-to-developer-handoff)).

11. **Grow the emotional range as the stakes grow.** Headspace moved "beyond a smile" to stress and sadness, and added photography for care ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)). Cash App became "more mature… lighter in touch" ([BUCK](https://www.buck.co/work/cash-app-visual-identity)).

12. **Restraint with no point of view also fails.** Slack's mark was "entirely lacking in soul" ([Web Designer Depot](https://www.webdesignerdepot.com/2019/01/slack-adopts-generic-new-identity/)). Cash App refused to become "another minimalistic brand" ([BUCK](https://www.buck.co/work/cash-app-visual-identity)). Taking out a loved character backfired at Cracker Barrel ([NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/return-of-the-old-timer-cracker-barrel-scraps-new-logo-following-backlash/6377097/)). But "a strong minority" still prefer calm ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)), so allow it.

## 2. Signs of cartoony versus signs of premium personality

**Signs of cartoony or cheap**

- Drawings on every screen, including the plain working screens.
- Many characters, or a mix of styles that do not match.
- Generic stock or "flat people" art that anyone can buy.
- Several bright colours fighting, with no quiet base.
- Jokes in errors, warnings, payments or moments of worry.
- Puns in headings that hide what the section holds.
- Animation on taps you make dozens of times, or animation that makes you wait.
- Icons with no labels, and patterns broken just to look new.
- A mascot that talks, nags or speaks for the brand everywhere.
- Cheer that does not match the user's real situation.

**Signs of premium personality**

- A calm, neutral base with one signature colour kept for one job.
- Personality placed at a few chosen moments, such as welcome, empty, done and celebrate.
- One character or one tight family, drawn by rules and shown sparingly.
- A clear silhouette and consistent craft, made by the team itself.
- Plain, warm, exact words, with humour that is dry and rare.
- Motion that explains or rewards, kept short.
- Emotion that changes with the moment, with no single forced smile.
- Delight that comes from the real content or data, like Weather's live sky.
- Labels kept, patterns kept, and clarity never traded for flair.
- A calmer option for people who want it.

## 3. What this means for a food and cooking app

None of my sources is about food. Each point below is my own reasoning from the cases above.

1. **Let the food be the colour and the delight.** Use real photos where Apple uses the real sky (principles 2 and 9). Give the app one signature colour on a calm base, and keep drawings out of the way of the food.

2. **Fence illustration to a few moments.** First run, empty collections or lists, no results, and finishing a recipe. Keep it off the recipe and cooking screens (principle 3).

3. **Treat food safety, allergens, cooking times and temperatures like money.** Use plain, exact words and no jokes. The same goes for errors and lost work (principles 4 and 5).

4. **Put personality into words and motion first.** Use warm, plain copy. Give a small reward at the end of a step or a recipe (principles 9 and 10). Any animation must stay short, because in a kitchen the hands are busy and the attention is split.

5. **If you use a character, use one with a job.** For example, one that guides a first-time cook. Keep it silent or rare, and give it more feelings than one smile, because cooking goes wrong too (principles 6, 7 and 11).

6. **Make the craft custom.** Bought or generated "flat people" art is what Corporate Memphis made cheap, and rich icons can now be copied by AI. Distinctiveness has to come from consistency and fit, not from style alone (principle 8).

7. **Offer calm.** Some users prefer less intensity. A cook in the middle of a recipe needs clarity more than excitement (principle 12).

8. **Note what the favourites actually do.** Lemonade and Headspace are restrained systems with one loud colour, a real voice, and personality kept at the edges. They are not illustrated everywhere.
