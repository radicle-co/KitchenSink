# Mainstream reference apps: what the evidence says

Researched 2026-10-20. This file asks which apps and sites that most people know are judged to have calm, clean, premium design, and why. It follows on from `bestApps.md` and `bestWebsites.md` in this folder, which covered small craft apps (Halide, Mela, Things, Linear, Stripe) and do not need repeating. Nothing here is a design proposal.

## How to read this

- **Two kinds of evidence.** A quote in "quote marks" comes from a page I opened and read. Some pages did not open (403, paywall, or a page that loads by script). For those, I only saw a search summary. I give such a claim without quote marks and tag it **(unverified snippet)**. Treat those as leads, not facts.
- **Counting rule.** I count agreement by **source family**, the same rule `bestApps.md` uses. Every Apple honour is one family (Apple Design Awards, App of the Year, Newsroom). A company's own design write-up is evidence of intent, not an independent judgement. Google's research on its own design system is Google's, not a third party's. A Webby **People's Voice** award is a public vote, so it counts for less than a jury award.
- **Single source** claims are marked **[single source]**.
- **What failed.**
    - Webby category pages load by script and showed no winners.
    - Bloomberg, Design Week, J.D. Power, 9to5Mac and Android Authority returned 403.
    - Forrester's 2025 UK banking ranking is behind a paywall.
    - Mobbin and Refero publish no popularity ranking I found. A screen library listing an app is not praise anyway, as `bestApps.md` already notes.
    - I found no Fast Company Innovation by Design honour for any app on the candidate list. Its 2025 General Excellence app was the New York Times app ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/innovation-by-design/2025)), which `bestApps.md` already records.

## The main finding

**Big mainstream apps rarely win design juries for their everyday interface.** A famous brand's design award is usually for a campaign, a one-off feature or a new platform. It is rarely for the screens people use every day:

- **Spotify.** Its D&AD credits are for **Wrapped**, a once-a-year campaign. Wrapped for Advertisers took a Wood Pencil in Digital Design in 2021. Wrapped Design was listed in 2024 ([D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/work/d-ad-awards-archive/wrapped-design)) **(unverified snippet for the pencil levels)**. The 2025 Webby went to a Spotify talk show ([Webby press release](https://www.webbyawards.com/press/press-releases/29th-annual-webby-awards-announce-2025-winners/)).
- **Duolingo, Netflix and Pinterest.** Their 2025 Webbys were for a video ("The Bird"), a live-event hub and a trend report. The release says: "Duolingo – "The Bird" won the Webby Award for Viral, Experimental & Weird". It also says "Pinterest Predicts 2025 won the Webby Award Best Trend, Insights or Impact Report" ([Webby](https://www.webbyawards.com/press/press-releases/29th-annual-webby-awards-announce-2025-winners/)).
- **Strava.** Its well-known design work is the yearly **Year in Sport** recap, which is a campaign. Manual made the 2020 edition. The studio said "The system needed to be simple to allow for animation to really bring the ideas to life" ([It's Nice That, 2021](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/manual-strava-year-in-sport-graphic-design-150321)).
- **Apple.** Its highest recent jury award was a D&AD **Black Pencil** in 2024. It was for **visionOS**, the Vision Pro software, not the iPhone ([Dezeen, 24 May 2024](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=2075601)).

So **a campaign award must not stand in for the app's interface.** That leaves two lists. The short one holds mainstream products with real, repeated praise for the everyday product. The long one holds famous looks with documented faults.

A second finding fits with this. J.D. Power's 2025 study of US banking apps reported that the gap between the best and worst apps was at its lowest level yet. It said most banks now build their apps on the same set of proven best practices ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250529002789/en/Bank-and-Credit-Card-Apps-and-Websites-Struggle-to-Stand-Out-J.D.-Power-Finds)) **(unverified snippet)**. In a crowded category, clean and competent is the minimum, not a difference.

## App by app

### Airbnb: the strongest mainstream case

**Families:** two independent honours, Google (Material Design Award, 2016) and Webby (jury, 2017, in a travel category rather than a design one). The 2025 coverage in It's Nice That and MacStories mostly relays Airbnb's own account, so it counts for less.

- **Google, 2016.** Airbnb won "a Material Design Award for focused efficiency in 2016." Google's reasons map straight onto calm and clean:
    - "Letting photography lead gives the UI a bold and uncluttered appearance."
    - "An emphasis on photography and ample white space helps people focus on essential information."
    - "The use of the sans-serif typeface Circular, conveys a friendly and warm character."
    - "Motion helps express the app's conversational tone."
      ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/airbnb-invites-you-in))
- **Webby, 2017.** "Airbnb won the Webby Award for Best Travel App for its New Airbnb App" ([Webby](https://www.webbyawards.com/press/press-releases/21st-annual-webby-award-winners-announced/)). A 2014 Webby for a travel app is also reported ([Webby](https://www.webbyawards.com/press/press-releases/18th-annual-webby-award-winners-announced/)) **(unverified snippet)**.
- **It's Nice That, 2025 redesign.** It describes "a softer feel across the board – more curved edges, and a smooth animated interface with subtle intensities." Teo Connor, VP of design, said the team "needed to enhance that, not get in the way of it". On the Trips tab, the aim was "not just easy to view … but a pleasure to look at." All of it was "done by an in-house team" ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/airbnb-app-redesign-140525)).
- **MacStories, 2025.** This is a podcast interview with Airbnb's leads, not an independent review. The post says "It's playful, welcoming and easy to use" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/linked/inside-airbnbs-app-redesign-an-appstories-interview-with-marketing-and-design-leads/)).
- **The 3D icons.** Bloomberg described Airbnb's new animated 3D icons as part of a wider move away from flat design. It put them beside Apple's Liquid Glass ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-13/apple-airbnb-ditch-flat-app-icons-for-new-3d-ui-design)) **(unverified snippet, page blocked)**.

**What makes it feel premium:** large photos lead the screen, with plenty of white space. It uses one warm rounded sans-serif (Circular, per Google). It adds soft corners, gentle motion, and a few richly made icons, used sparingly.

**Criticism:** I found no strong press criticism of the 2025 redesign. Host forum posts complain that some tasks now take more taps ([Airbnb community](https://community.withairbnb.com/t5/Ask-about-your-listing/Changes-on-hosts-webpage/m-p/2227815)) **(unverified snippet, one user)**. Older UX case studies cite fees that appear late in checkout **(unverified snippet)**. That is a pricing-honesty fault, not a visual one, but it is part of the experience.

### Apple: a source of principles, with documented faults

**What the evidence covers.** The positive evidence is for visionOS (D&AD jury), one apple.com product page (Webby public vote), and Apple's own stated principles. **None of it is independent praise for the iPhone apps.** For those apps, the independent evidence in this file is criticism (NN/g, TidBITS, the tech press). Read this entry as a source of principles, not an endorsement of the iPhone apps.

- **Praise.**
    - At the visionOS Black Pencil, Alan Dye, then Apple's VP of Human Interface Design, said "Our goal has always been to create intuitive user experiences that blur the lines between hardware and software" ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=2075601)).
    - apple.com's iMac page won the 2025 **People's Voice** award in Best Visual Design – Function ([Webby](https://winners.webbyawards.com/winners/websites-and-mobile-sites/features-design/best-visual-design-function)). That is a public vote.
    - Envato reports Apple Fitness+ as a 2025 Webby winner for Best User Experience ([Envato](https://elements.envato.com/learn/webby-awards)). The official press release I read does not list it. Treat it as **unconfirmed**.
- **Apple's stated aim for Liquid Glass.** It is "a new material" that "reflects and refracts its surroundings", aimed at "bringing greater focus to content". Dye, then still at Apple: "It combines the optical qualities of glass with a fluidity only Apple can achieve" ([Apple Newsroom, June 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/)).
- **The best-documented criticism of any product in this file.** Raluca Budiu of the Nielsen Norman Group ([NN/g, 10 Oct 2025](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)):
    - "the contrast between the text and the background is often too low"
    - "the interface is shouting "look at me" when it should quietly step aside"
    - "Motion for motion's sake is not usability"
    - "Apple is prioritizing spectacle over usability"
- **Apple's response, and what it showed.** Apple added a **Tinted** option in iOS 26.1. TidBITS found the Clear lock screen had "so much white in the upper third that "Enter Passcode" and dots are completely invisible", while "the Tinted version reduces the overall brightness to make everything readable" ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2025/10/21/ios-26-1-to-add-optional-opacity-to-liquid-glass/)). The maker shipped an opt-out within months. That is strong proof of the fault.
- **Photos (iOS 18).** Mark Gurman reported Apple judged the redesign "probably a little too bold and potentially confusing". In beta, Apple "pushed albums higher up into the app to require less scrolling" and "moved saved content back into the main photo grid" ([iMore, 11 Aug 2024](https://imore.com/apps/user-backlash-has-reportedly-caused-apple-to-rethink-ios-18s-photos-redesign)). Months after release, Creative Bloq's headline was still "I thought Apple's iPhone Photos app redesign would grow on people... boy, was I wrong!" ([Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/tech/we-thought-the-apple-photos-redesign-would-grow-on-people-boy-were-we-were-wrong)).
- **Weather, Journal, Invites.**
    - Weather: a TechRadar writer called its interface cluttered and not particularly user-friendly ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/ios/ios-18-gives-the-apple-weather-app-two-cool-upgrades-but-i-still-miss-dark-sky)) **(unverified snippet)**.
    - Journal: MacStories called it beautiful, while The Sweet Setup said it did not feel fully finished ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/apples-journal-app-journaling-for-all/), [The Sweet Setup](https://thesweetsetup.com/?p=164530)) **(unverified snippets)**.
    - Invites: reviewers called it clean, and Partiful called it derivative ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2598411/apples-new-app-invites-copycat-criticism.html)) **(unverified snippet)**.
- **Music, TV app, Fitness, Wallet.** I found no current design review strong enough to cite either way.

**What to take:** content first, one clear hierarchy, system type at generous sizes, depth used to separate layers. **What to avoid:** see-through panels over busy photos, low-contrast text, and motion that draws the eye for its own sake. Apple is the best example of both the principle and the failure, often in the same release.

### Headspace and Calm: the calm category

- **Headspace.** It is in `bestApps.md` (ADA finalist 2022, winner 2023), so here is one line. Apple praised its "thoughtful design decisions, an incredible UI, and a recognizable, comfortable illustration style", and said it is "remarkably easy to navigate (collections are clearly labeled with understandable purposes)" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2023/)). It has one family, but the praise repeats over two years and Apple says its standard has held for "More than a decade". Its 2025 Webby People's Voice award was for Headspace XR, not the phone app ([Webby](https://www.webbyawards.com/press/press-releases/29th-annual-webby-awards-announce-2025-winners/)).
- **Calm.** Its App Store listing says it was Apple's 2017 iPhone App of the Year ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/calm-sleep-meditation/id571800810)) **(unverified snippet, self-reported)**. The only design reviews I found were student critiques. They describe a minimal home screen built around a looping nature image ([Pratt IXD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2018/01/design-critique-calm-ios-app/)) **(unverified snippet, weak source)**.

**What makes it calm:** soft illustration or nature imagery, few choices per screen, clear labels, slow pacing.

### Duolingo: excellent craft, but not calm

- **Apple, 2023 Delight and Fun winner.** "the Duolingo team embarked on a comprehensive redesign of both interface and content, making it even easier and more enjoyable to start learning a new language" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2023/)).
- **The 2019 rebrand.** Johnson Banks rebuilt the identity around a custom typeface, Feather Bold. The aim was to fix pieces that had grown up in an ad hoc way ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/duolingo-rebrand-johnson-banks/)) **(unverified snippet)**.
- **Criticism.** A 2025 academic thesis studied Duolingo's "bright and deceptive patterns". It points to too many notifications sent to keep people engaged, despite a clear interface ([UFRPE](https://arandu.ufrpe.br/items/e55879b3-a7af-4aa1-839b-c91f56e1fbcd/full)) **(unverified snippet)**. Apple's own citation celebrates the "learning streak" and the "endearingly tenacious mascot". Those are the same mechanics critics call guilt.

**What to take:** one consistent illustration and type system, and bright feedback when a task is done. **What not to take:** the pressure.

### Monzo: a bank people describe as simple

**The evidence is thin, and partly self-reported.**

- **Its own leaders.** In an It's Nice That interview, chief design officer Vuokko Aro named what Monzo will always keep: "Simple, modern product design" and "The hot coral colour" ([It's Nice That, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/in-house-monzo-vuokko-aro-coral-garvey-creative-industry-020926)). That is the company describing itself.
- **Forrester.** Monzo led Forrester's UK banking CX Index in 2021. It fell to fourth in 2022 and stayed in the top four in 2023 ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/highlights-from-uk-banking-cx-index-2023-how-do-uk-banking-brands-earn-loyalty)) **(unverified snippet)**. That measures the whole customer experience, not visual design.
- **British Bank Awards.** Monzo's App Store listing cites a 2023 Best Banking App win ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/monzo-bank-mobile-banking/id1052238659)) **(unverified snippet, self-reported, public vote)**.

**What to take:** one signature colour used with restraint against navy and soft white, and plain-English money screens.

### Google, Material 3 Expressive: evidence of method, mixed on result

- **Google's own research, reported by Dezeen.** Google ran "a total of 46 studies with over 18,000 participants". Key elements were spotted "up to four times faster in M3 Expressive versus Material 3". The designs "erased the usability age gap", and the system adds "a new physics-informed motion system". Google's own designer added a useful limit: "you might not want a super-playful UI for paying a parking ticket" ([Dezeen, May 2025](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/05/28/google-ushers-in-age-of-expressive-interfaces-with-material-design-update/)). This is Google grading itself. I found no independent replication.
- **The press verdict.** "M3 Expressive is an absolute success on phones, tablets, and watches", but the Google apps feel "like Material 3.5", and "how apps work following the redesign does not feel any more cohesive than before, and that's a missed opportunity" ([9to5Google, Dec 2025](https://9to5google.com/2025/12/27/recap-material-3-expressive/)).

**What to take:** the method. Test that the primary action is easy to find, and check with older users. It is also a reminder that "expressive" has to be dosed: calm screens for serious tasks.

### Threads: clean by subtraction [single source]

- "It's very simple, and I like that, but I'm not sure if that's due to the rushed launch or by design." The same review says "The main Instagram app is very cluttered, not just with content, given how much functionality is being packed into it" ([9to5Google, July 2023](https://9to5google.com/2023/07/07/threads-android-app-brief-review/)).
- Launch-era commentators also called Threads decluttered compared with Instagram ([Viral Nation](https://www.viralnation.com/?p=151910)) **(unverified snippet)**.

**What to take:** one column, text first, almost no chrome. It also shows that a sibling app can be calm while the parent app, Instagram, is not.

### Uber (2019): a lesson in fighting feature creep [single source]

- In 2019 the redesign "simplified parts of the Uber look and feel", with "a minimal grid showing the range of services". The in-house aim was to "avoid the app becoming cluttered, hard to navigate, and cumbersome" ([It's Nice That, 27 Sep 2019](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/uber-redesign-digital-270919)).
- Earlier, Fortune had described the old ride menu as a crowded mess ([Fortune, 2016](https://fortune.com/2016/11/02/uber-app-redesign)) **(unverified snippet)**.
- Later, Uber pushed ad notifications that users complained about ([TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/31/uber-tests-push-notifications-a-feature-literally-no-one-wants/embed/)) **(unverified snippet)**.

**What to take:** one question first (where are you going?). Then the warning: advertising and new services, piled on, wear calm away.

## Looks premium, carries known faults

1. **Liquid Glass (iOS 26).** Low contrast over busy backgrounds and attention-seeking motion. This is shown by NN/g, by TidBITS, and by Apple's own Tinted opt-out (sources above).
2. **Apple Photos (iOS 18).** One long scroll, with albums buried, then partly rolled back (iMore, Creative Bloq, above).
3. **Spotify home feed (2023).** Two headlines: "Spotify's redesign is so bad I'm considering switching to Apple Music" ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/opinion/spotifys-redesign-is-so-bad-im-considering-switching-to-apple-music)). And "Was this Spotify redesign totally necessary?" ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/spotify-home-redesign)). The complaints were a TikTok-style feed with autoplay, one item at a time, and an app trying to do too much at once ([Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/opinion/the-spotify-redesign-is-a-mess-4312654), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/03/09/spotify-redesign-apple-music/)) **(unverified snippets for the body text)**.
4. **Netflix autoplay and the 2025 TV home screen.** Netflix added an opt-out for autoplay previews in February 2020, after years of complaints. It said, in effect, that some people find the feature helpful and others do not ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20200207-turn-off-netflix-autoplay)) **(unverified snippet)**. The 2025 TV redesign uses bigger tiles and moved genre browsing to Search. It drew a petition and also a defence. The defence's headline: "Netflix's latest redesign has sparked some criticism online — but I'm here to defend it" ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/entertainment/netflix/netflixs-updated-homepage-is-getting-pushback-from-some-users-heres-why-its-exactly-what-the-service-needs)). The details are an **(unverified snippet)**.
5. **Robinhood.** It once won an Apple Design Award ([Mother Jones](https://motherjones.com/media/2021/10/robinhood-trading-app-design-ui-ux-video)) **(unverified snippet)**. Then Massachusetts regulators cited "its use of gamification strategies to manipulate customers", and the confetti was removed ([NBC Boston, 31 Mar 2021](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/business/money-report/robinhood-gets-rid-of-confetti-feature-amid-scrutiny-over-gamification-of-investing/2343214/)). It is the clearest case of polish used against users.
6. **Duolingo's streak pressure.** Lovely craft, guilt mechanics (above).
7. **Instagram.** "very cluttered" (9to5Google, above).
8. **Revolut.** Its own forum users counted 23 buttons and links on the home screen. They called it cluttered ([Revolut community](https://community.revolut.com/t/ui-overhaul/147896?page=8)) **(unverified snippet, forum evidence only)**. It shows how a "super app" loses calm.

The common pattern: **a premium surface does not survive density, autoplay, ads or engagement tricks.**

## (1) Ranked shortlist of mainstream references

The table above is ranked by evidence. The shortlist below makes one stated exception for tone, as `bestApps.md` did: Duolingo is loud, not calm, so it moves to last and counts for craft only.

1. **Airbnb.** _Evidence:_ Material Design Award 2016, Webby 2017, plus It's Nice That and MacStories on the 2025 redesign. _Take:_ photography leads, ample white space, one warm rounded sans, soft corners, subtle motion, a few rich icons rather than many plain ones.
2. **Headspace.** _Evidence:_ ADA 2022 finalist and 2023 winner (one family, but held for a decade, per Apple). _Take:_ a comfortable illustration style, clearly labelled collections, few choices per screen.
3. **Threads [single source].** _Take:_ a single text-first column with almost no chrome. The contrast with Instagram is the warning.
4. **Uber, 2019 [single source].** _Take:_ one primary question on the first screen, and a minimal grid for the rest.
5. **Google Material 3 Expressive.** _Evidence:_ Google's 46 studies (its own research, reported by Dezeen) and 9to5Google's "absolute success" on devices. _Take:_ the habit of testing that people find the primary action fast, including older users. Keep expressiveness out of serious tasks.
6. **Monzo [weak evidence].** _Evidence:_ Forrester first place in 2021 (snippet) and its own design leaders. _Take:_ one signature colour with restraint, and plain money screens.
7. **Apple, for principles only.** _Evidence:_ D&AD Black Pencil 2024 (visionOS) and Webby People's Voice 2025 (apple.com). This is not evidence for the iPhone apps, where NN/g and TidBITS document faults. _Take:_ content first, strong hierarchy, generous system type, layers that show depth. _Do not take:_ glass over busy content, or text contrast below the standard.
8. **Duolingo, for craft only.** _Evidence:_ ADA 2023. _Take:_ a consistent character and type system, and clear feedback when a task is done. Leave the streak pressure behind.

## (2) Premium-looking, but with known faults to avoid

- **Apple Liquid Glass**: legibility and motion (NN/g, TidBITS, Apple's opt-out).
- **Apple Photos, iOS 18**: hidden structure and endless scroll (iMore, Creative Bloq).
- **Spotify home feed**: a vertical autoplay feed, one item at a time, doing too much (Tom's Guide, Creative Bloq).
- **Netflix**: autoplay previews, and a 2025 TV home screen that favours passive scrolling (opt-out added in 2020, Tom's Guide).
- **Robinhood**: gamified polish cited by a regulator (NBC Boston).
- **Instagram and Revolut**: density from adding features (9to5Google, forum evidence).
- **Duolingo**: guilt-driven notifications (academic critique, snippet).

## (3) What the shortlist shares

1. **Content leads, chrome recedes.** Airbnb lets "photography lead". Apple's stated aim is "greater focus to content". NN/g's complaint is the inverse: an interface that should "quietly step aside".
2. **Plenty of white space.** Google credits Airbnb's "ample white space" with helping people "focus on essential information".
3. **One warm, readable type family.** Circular at Airbnb, Feather Bold at Duolingo, system type at Apple. Few sizes, used firmly.
4. **Contrast is never sacrificed for effect.** Liquid Glass shows that premium materials fail the moment text gets hard to read.
5. **One signature colour, used sparingly.** Monzo's hot coral against navy and soft white. Duolingo's green.
6. **Soft geometry.** Airbnb's "more curved edges" and Google's shape library both soften the screen without adding noise.
7. **Motion that is subtle and has a purpose.** "subtle intensities" (Airbnb), not "Motion for motion's sake" (NN/g).
8. **One clear first question per screen.** Uber's single destination question, Headspace's "clearly labeled" collections, and Google's finding that people find the key action faster.
9. **Restraint over time.** The mainstream apps that lost calm did it by adding things: feeds, autoplay, ads, services, streak pressure. Calm is kept by refusing additions, not won once.
10. **No dark patterns.** Polish used to push engagement is the opposite of premium. The faults list shows it gets called out (Robinhood, Duolingo streaks, Netflix autoplay).

**Limits.** Most quotes came through a fetch tool that summarises pages. Short quotes match the fetched text, but I did not read every page in full. Several key pages (Bloomberg, J.D. Power, Android Authority, the bodies of the Spotify and Netflix pieces) did not open, and those claims are tagged. I found no user survey that ranks mainstream apps on visual design specifically. The rankings above come from juries, the press and expert reviews, not from measured user preference.
