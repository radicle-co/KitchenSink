Ideation

Monetizing free users

Forty-three candidates across six frames, filtered to seven. The strongest finding is not an idea at all — it is a contradiction already sitting in the specs.

Date 2026-08-28 Topic free-user-monetization Focus how do we monetize free users? Mode repo-grounded

Grounding Context

Codebase context. Commise is a TypeScript monorepo shipping a recipe / meal-planning / cooking platform to web (Next.js 15) and mobile (Expo 57), on AWS with PostgreSQL 18. Capabilities: recipe management and import, meal planning, grocery lists, cooking mode, and USDA-backed nutrition. A chef creator marketplace is being specced separately.

The recorded business model (docs/business-plan.md) names three revenue streams: Instacart commission at 5% of every grocery cart ordered through the app ($8K/mo at month 6 → $100K/mo at month 12); subscriptions; and creator/CPG sponsorship. The North Star metric is weekly grocery orders through the app. The recorded arithmetic: a user ordering twice a month at $85/order generates $8.50/month in commission against a $5.99 subscription.

Two business plans in the repo disagree. docs/business-plan.md prices premium at $5.99/month and calls commission primary; specs/executive/05-business-plan.md frames commerce as later-stage “upside”; and 010-FR-041 prices premium at $6.99/month. One of these is stale and a spec cites the other. Worth reconciling regardless of what is decided here.

Ratified positions that bound every candidate. “No ads in the free tier” is a documented competitive moat — ad-supported free tiers are the #1 complaint across recipe apps, and a named competitor is recorded as “how NOT to monetize.” The competitive analysis recommends the inverse of tightening: “hold or raise price, and buy the position with a far more generous free tier.” 015-FR-027 forbids artificial scarcity and false urgency; EU DSA Art. 25 prohibits manipulative interface design outright. Allergen, dietary-restriction and household-composition data may never be monetized. Selling user data, paywalling public recipes, retroactively removing free-tier capability, and licensing the corpus for AI training are all out.

Known blockers. 010-FR-044 is unshipped, so subscription tier is not a signed session-token claim and no service outside identity can enforce a premium gate today. Feature 007 records that no partner API access is confirmed with Walmart or Instacart. The ChatGPT Custom GPT channel (200M+ weekly ChatGPT users) has no per-user tracking or attribution path.

External research covered health-payer B2B2C (Foodsmart's $33 PMPM; Noom's PBM-funded GLP-1 companion; Instacart Health produce prescriptions in ~24 states), appliance-OEM licensing (SideChef's non-exclusive survival against Yummly's single-owner shutdown), B2B recipe/nutrition API licensing, Ibotta-style brand-funded cash back, lead-generation price anchors, and the reCAPTCHA/Duolingo disclosure failure mode.

1.

Un-gate the transaction, gate the automation

A3 · Crossing to paidConfidence 92%Complexity Lowbasis: direct
Description

Move every transactional capability into the free tier — online ordering, cart handoff, retailer connection, and the pack-size and waste math that raises basket quality — and redraw the premium line around automation and inference: AI generation, auto-plans, instruction optimization, trainer planning. That is also where marginal cost actually sits.

Basis — direct, verified across three documents

010-FR-041 lists “online grocery ordering” among the premium unlocks. 007-FR-031 marks the checkout handoff “(Premium — requires FR-030…)”. 010-FR-040 gives the free tier “grocery list generation” but not ordering. And docs/business-plan.md:258 states: “The subscription is the upsell, not the primary revenue. Free users who use Instacart generate more revenue than paid subscribers who don't.”

Rationale

The specs paywall the exact mechanism the business plan names as primary revenue, so only the small paying minority is structurally permitted to generate it. Every other candidate here — rebates, benefit dollars, anonymous carts, intent signal — is downstream of a free user being able to transact. And the compounding runs the right way: cart frequency is habit-shaped, so an order enabled in month one is an annuity, whereas a subscription blocked in month one is nothing.

Downsides

It removes the free tier's most concrete upgrade trigger, so subscription conversion may fall before commission rises — and the recorded 5% → 8% conversion target would need restating rather than quietly missing. 010 and 007 must be amended together. It also promotes the unconfirmed retailer API from a premium nicety to a first-order free-tier dependency, which sharpens rather than softens that risk.

2.

Brand-funded rebate paid to the user, settled on the receipt

A1 · The grocery basketConfidence 78%Complexity Highbasis: external5 of 6 frames converged
Description

A CPG brand funds a rebate on an item already on the user's meal-plan list. The user opts in, shops wherever they normally shop — in store or delivered — and the rebate settles by receipt capture or card link. The cash goes to the user; Commise takes a facilitation fee. No impression, no placement, no retailer API.

Basis — external

Ibotta: brands pay 1–3% of transaction value for a redeemed offer and the user receives the cash — structurally an offer-redemption fee, not an ad placement. Set against 007's record that no partner API is confirmed, and the ratified no-ads moat.

Rationale

Five of six frames arrived here independently, which is itself signal. It monetizes the roughly 85% of US grocery spend that never touches a delivery integration, it survives Instacart never signing, and it threads the no-ads moat structurally rather than by argument. Every redemption is also a verified purchase event, which upgrades the platform's intent signal from “planned” to “bought” — the closed loop that would make any later commerce-media position sellable at a premium.

Downsides

Receipt capture and card-linking are each their own consent surface and their own fraud surface. The offer must be strictly additive to the recipe's ingredient and never a silent substitution, or it corrodes the nutrition-truth differentiator the free tier exists to prove. And rebate networks are a scale business: the brand-side supply needs a partner or a long build before the first dollar moves.

3.

Benefit-dollar checkout — the member carries the payer's money

A2 · Third partiesConfidence 70%Complexity Highbasis: external5 of 6 frames converged
Description

Make a meal plan spendable against food-benefit instruments the user already holds — FSA/HSA, Medicare Advantage OTC and Healthy-Food cards, SNAP EBT Online, produce-prescription allowances. Flag which grocery-list lines are eligible and route checkout to a rail that accepts the card. Revenue is a per-basket facilitation fee from the administrator or the rail. The member pays nothing and enrolls themselves.

Basis — external

Instacart Health reports ~24 states covering produce prescriptions under Medicaid 1115/ILOS waivers, and 2026 Medicare Advantage OTC/Healthy-Food cards integrate with Instacart checkout. Foodsmart contracts with Medicaid and Exchange plans citing $33 PMPM downstream savings — roughly five times the subscription price — with the member receiving nutrition support free.

Rationale

Five frames converged, and it is the only candidate where the segment least able to pay is a segment someone else is already paying for. The member-carries-the-money shape matters: it reaches the same money as a PMPM plan contract without the eighteen-month enterprise sale this team has no capacity to run. It also stacks with the basket rather than competing with it — the benefit card and the grocery cart are the same checkout.

Downsides

Knowing a user holds a Healthy-Food card is health-adjacent, and the hard stop forbids monetizing that class of data — so the design must monetize the transaction and never the eligibility signal, and that boundary has to be drawn before anything is built, not asserted afterwards. Benefit rails are heavily regulated with a large integration surface, and the eligibility mapping has to be maintained against changing program rules.

4.

Anonymous attributed cart handoff

A5 · Where free users arriveConfidence 75%Complexity Mediumbasis: direct + reasoned4 of 6 frames converged
Description

The Custom GPT's terminal action stops being “install the app” and becomes a signed, durable, shoppable cart URL carrying the affiliate identifier. No account, no cookie, no identity resolution. Make the artifact claimable, so that if the person later signs up, the recipes, list and order history attach retroactively.

Basis — direct, then reasoned

Direct: the channel reaches 200M+ weekly ChatGPT users and “no per-GPT-user tracking or attribution path exists yet”; the month-6 target is 20,000 GPT users producing 5,000 grocery orders. Reasoned: affiliate commission attribution has never required user identity — it requires only that the transaction carry a partner tag. So the missing attribution blocks the subscription upsell, not the commission line, which is the primary revenue stream. Building identity first is therefore backwards.

Rationale

Four frames converged, and the insight reorders the roadmap: the GPT channel's blocker is not the missing tracking. It also drops the metered ten-recipe cap that the competitive analysis calls self-defeating, and it generalizes — the same claimable-cart endpoint is what any other agent surface would call, which means distribution can be given away while revenue is collected on carts, with no need for the agent platform to agree to pay anything.

Downsides

Transacting for someone who has no account raises real consumer-protection questions — who receives the receipt, who can seek a refund, and who the buyer of record is. It depends on the same retailer integration as everything else on the basket axis. And a durable signed URL that carries a commission identifier is a token-leak and affiliate-fraud surface that has to be designed for.

5.

Meter AI, don't gate it

A3 · Crossing to paidConfidence 80%Complexity Mediumbasis: direct
Description

Let every free user reach every AI capability, and charge per action from a small prepaid credit balance priced at measured cost plus margin, with the real per-call cost shown. Subscribers get an included monthly allotment instead of an on/off switch. A softer variant: free users get the same capability on a deferred overnight queue, and what premium buys is immediacy and iteration.

Basis — direct

010-FR-044 is unshipped, so no service outside identity can enforce a premium gate today — every gating idea is blocked by construction, and a metering idea is not. Meanwhile ADR-0024 already ships a per-call reserve-then-settle dollar counter in production, keyed to a period, charging worst case before the call and refunding after. The competitive analysis separately calls gating at 5–10 items per month “self-defeating.”

Rationale

This is the only free/paid design in the set that is buildable this quarter, because it needs the counter that already exists rather than the session-token claim that does not. It moves AI from a gate to a purchasable, which is the direction the competitive analysis explicitly recommends; it monetizes users who will never subscribe but will spend two dollars once; and it makes the marginal-cost risk of a generous free tier bounded and observable rather than feared. Because it adds capability to the free tier, it cannot read as a punishing paywall.

Downsides

Prepaid balances are a payments, refund and expiry surface of their own, and consumable purchases carry their own platform-store commission treatment on mobile. Per-action pricing can feel worse than a subscription for heavy users, so the included allotment has to be sized carefully or it produces exactly the resentment it was meant to avoid. One thing to verify before committing: recipe-workers appears to have no service-to-service credential to identity today, which the deferred-queue variant would need.

6.

Corrections as a costed, disclosed contribution economy

A4 · What free users makeConfidence 82%Complexity Mediumbasis: direct + external3 of 6 frames converged
Description

Free users already produce ingredient parse corrections, which promote from personal to global once enough distinct users corroborate them. Instrument that loop as a costed asset — dollars of avoided inference per promoted correction — disclose it plainly in-product, and pay contributors in premium time.

Basis — direct, with an external failure mode

ADR-0027: the partial unique index on (normalized_key, user_id) is the distinct-user counter that promotes a correction from personal to global — the corroboration machinery already exists and already runs on free-user labour. ADR-0024 puts a hard $100/month ceiling on inference with per-call-site attribution, so a promoted correction carries a literal, computable saving. External: reCAPTCHA and Duolingo's crowdsourced translation drew lasting criticism for extracting value from free users without disclosure — the failure mode is silence, not the extraction.

Rationale

Three frames converged. This is the only mechanism here whose economics improve with free-tier scale, and it is already running — unmeasured. Measuring it converts “are we giving free users too much” from an argument into an evidence question, and it lets the free tier widen truthfully, as inference cost per import genuinely falls, rather than as an act of generosity nobody can defend at a budget review.

Downsides

Paying for corrections creates an incentive to submit noise, so the corroboration threshold and an anti-gaming rule have to be designed before launch rather than patched after. Premium-time rewards cost compute rather than cash, but they are still a cost against the same capped pool. And the disclosure has to lead the feature, not trail it — the cited failure mode is precisely the platform that took the work quietly.

7.

Appliance and trade lead-gen

A2 · Third partiesConfidence 62%Complexity Mediumbasis: external + reasoned
Description

A cook's behaviour reveals the durable graph around the food — what equipment they own, what they cannot make without it, and when something fails mid-recipe. Offer a user-initiated referral (“find someone”) to appliance repair, replacement or installation. Commise is paid for the introduction; no record is ever sold.

Basis — external, then reasoned

External: Angi/HomeAdvisor shared leads run $15–$120+, with appliance-adjacent trades at the top of that band and cost per booked job at $250–$500. Reasoned: the recorded model values an engaged free user at $8.50/month of grocery commission — roughly $100/year at full engagement. A single appliance-repair introduction is a meaningful fraction of that from one event, and a replacement referral exceeds it outright. Equipment presence is already captured for a first-class product reason, because cooking mode must know what a recipe requires.

Rationale

It is the only candidate here that is not a food-margin business, which makes it uncorrelated with the grocery-partner risk every other idea on the basket axis inherits. And it has an intent moment nothing else in the funnel can match: the appliance that fails mid-cook is the highest-urgency commercial signal this product will ever observe. It is also distinct from the already-catalogued equipment affiliate, which sells products at single-digit retail rates — trade services are a lead market with an entirely different clearing price.

Downsides

Lead generation has a deserved reputation problem: this must be strictly user-initiated and never a sold record, or it corrodes the trust position the whole free tier is built on. It is a different vertical with its own supply-side sourcing and quality control, which is real operating work. And the mid-cook failure moment — the one with the most commercial energy — is exactly when interrupting a user is most hostile, so the affordance has to be found rather than pushed.
