## 1. Summary

A cook pastes a recipe. Some ingredients match a real food; some do not. Today the ones that do not are
indistinguishable from each other — the cook sees no reason, gets no remedy, and cannot fix the row
without deleting it.

They cannot be told why, because **nothing keeps the reason**. The resolution cascade works it out and
throws it away. That one fact is why this is a data change and not a UI change.

Fixing it exposed three more problems in the same area: the recipe database keeps a **shadow copy of the
food catalog**, the recipe service sits in the middle of **ingredient search** for no reason, and the catalog **names
foods the way a laboratory does, not the way a cook does**. All three are addressed here, by owner ruling —
the third by §4.7's FoodOn roots and `food_variant`.

## 2. Actors

| ID     | Actor                          | Interest                                                                                                    |
| ------ | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A1** | **Cook (recipe author)**       | Wants every ingredient to carry real food data. When one does not, wants to know why and fix it in place.   |
| **A2** | **Cook (recipe viewer)**       | Reads someone else's recipe. Must never see another cook's private food, nor an error that is not theirs.   |
| **A3** | **Second cook, independently** | Resolves the same phrase. Their agreement promotes a correction from personal to shared.                    |
| **A4** | **Operator**                   | Needs to answer "why is nothing resolving" without a log search inside a retention window.                  |
| **A5** | **Food service**               | Sole owner of food names and nutrition. Owns the ingested catalog, the remote source search, and the label. |

## 3. User stories

### US1 — I can see why an ingredient did not match

As **A1**, when an ingredient has no food, I see an error icon in the row. Activating it explains _this_
row's cause in plain words and offers something I can act on. On mobile I tap it; nothing is hover-only.

### US2 — I can fix the name in place

As **A1**, an unmatched ingredient's name is editable where it sits. It does not look like a bordered
input until I focus it. The same search-as-you-type used when adding an ingredient offers matches. When
I commit, we record the correction and try again. If it fails, the correction is withdrawn, I see the
new reason, and I can try again.

### US3 — My correction helps the next cook

As **A3**, when I independently resolve the same phrase to the same food, that agreement promotes the
correction from personal to shared. After that, a cook typing either phrase resolves without a lookup.

### US4 — I can make it my own food

As **A1**, when the name is right and the catalog simply lacks it, I can create a private food I own
from that row. The row then matches it. That control sits **in the row**, not in a popover.

### US5 — I can see nutrition for a matched ingredient

As **A1/A2**, a matched ingredient shows an information icon in the same place the error icon would be.
Activating it shows nutrition and food details, including calories. Nutrition loads in the background
after the recipe loads; when it has not arrived, the popover shows a loading state.

### US6 — I can remove an ingredient

As **A1**, deleting an ingredient from my recipe removes only that row. It never affects another recipe.

### US7 — I can fix a WRONG match

As **A1**, when a row matched the wrong food, I can reopen the picker and choose a different one —
**keeping the amount, unit, prep note and section**. Without this the only escape is deleting the row
and retyping it. ⚠️ **This story was missing from the first version of this plan.** It is the owner's
explicit _"we also need to be able to reopen the picker"_, and it is the remedy the origin design calls
**"Change food"**.

### US8 — I can find my recipes by food

As **A1**, I filter my recipes by picking a **food**, then seeing recipes that use it. ⛔ Not free-text
search over ingredient text — we have nothing that tokenises food names or supports natural language.

### US9 — Ingredient search does not depend on the recipe service

As **A1**, typing in the ingredient field searches the food catalog and the remote source directly. A
recipe-service deploy does not interrupt it.

⛔ **OWNER RULING, 2026-09-22. THIS SECTION REVERSES ITS OWN PREVIOUS CONTENT.** An earlier draft

On `ham (cured)` that is a **195%** error rendered as a clean value. ⛔ Worse, the collapse is SILENT: `bulkSeed.service.ts` dedups on `normalizeName` and its own docblock says the second row "collapses onto the first's row instead of raising `23505`", so the seeder reports success. ⚠️ `food_popularity` (PK `food_id`, `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`) loses 89 of 90 consumption priors the same way while logging 90 writes. ✅ **Resolved by §16 item 1** (owner rulings and a verified two-table design); this banner records why R56 as first written could not be built. ⚠️ This is forced, not chosen: **FoodOn contains ZERO nutrient values**, verified nine ways including its partner ontology CDNO (also zero numeric literals), its live OLS4 API, and its own site — which promises "vocabulary **for** nutritional analysis" and future-tense "**links to**" FDC. ⛔ A root therefore CANNOT exist without at least one source item to elect. |
| **R57** | ⛔ **Matching a source description to a root is restricted to the UN-INVERTED forms** — the full description, all comma segments reversed, and the first two segments un-inverted (`Cheese, cheddar` → `cheddar cheese`). ⛔ **Head-noun-alone matching is BANNED.** It offers 801 further matches and every one is truncation: it files `Bread, salvadoran sweet cheese` and `Bread, white wheat` both under `bread`. That is §6.6 failures 1 and 3 in a new coat. ⚠️ The un-inverted first two segments name only the FIRST part of a blend or a mix: `Oil, corn, peanut, and olive` → `corn oil`, `Bologna, chicken, turkey, pork` → `chicken bologna` (14 of the 306 matches in `docs/reports/2026-09-22/foodOnMappingReview.csv`). So every R57 match is `added`, and R54b's review approves it before it groups. |

A **kind arm** means the kind is known, and the line uses that kind's nutrition. A root with no kinds is always bound on the root arm. | **R60** | ⛔ FoodOn is **vendored, not called at request time**. ⚠️ Its release tagging has lapsed — newest git tag `v2025-07-31`, while master declares `2026-09-20` and every public service serves master — so a version **cannot be pinned** upstream. We snapshot master, record its `versionIRI` **and sha256** beside the ingest, and bump deliberately with a diff review. Change detection is an **ETag conditional GET** against the PURL (verified: `304`, zero bytes). |
| **R61** | _(figures: `docs/reports/2026-09-22/foodOnCoverageV2.py`)_ ⚠️ **Coverage is 57%, and the remainder is a curation list with no owner named.** USDA's own cross-reference names 4,126 of 7,793 SR Legacy foods (53%); FoodOn's vocabulary safely adds 341 more. ⛔ **AND THE UNCOVERED 43% IS NOT RANDOM — IT IS THE STAPLES, BECAUSE R57 EXCLUDES THEM.** `bread` (n=50), `chicken` (58), `salad dressing` (57) and `infant formula` (100) reach a FoodOn label ONLY through the head-noun form R57 bans. ⚠️ So "FoodOn HAS the label" and "a source staple safely MATCHES it" are different claims, and only the first is true for these. ## 6. The measured problem

Measured 2026-09-20 by reading the files named. A dated snapshot, not a maintained list.

### 6.1 Three impossible-to-trust states are currently possible

The food reference is nullable while the status can read "matched" beside it — a claim that a lookup
succeeded on a row holding no result. Whether the cook typed the name is a stored flag nothing ties to
the binding; a typed-in row badged "matched" has already shipped. And the recipe row holds a copy of a
name owned one table over, written by a different statement, with nothing keeping the two in step.

### 6.2 The reason is worked out and thrown away — this is the blocker

The cascade returns which tiers it consulted and which were unavailable. The caller discards all of it
the moment the outcome is not a match. The resolutions table records successes only. Diagnostics live
on a parse-job table that expires.

⛔ **This is why the requested feature cannot be built today.** There is no reason to show, because
nothing keeps one.

### 6.3 A shadow copy of the food catalog

The recipe database holds a name column with its own search index and three generated ranking columns —
a second copy of the food table with its own drift. The table's own comment says it holds the reference
"and nothing derived from it". It was violating itself.

### 6.4 The search proxy adds less than it looks — but not nothing

Both things the typeahead needs already exist in food-service: the ingested catalog and the remote source
search.

⚠️ **An earlier version of this section was wrong twice, and the correction matters.** The phrase
"user-agnostic by design" is **food-service's**, about its own route — it was misattributed to
recipe-service. And the recipe-service routes are not pure pass-throughs: one scopes private foods by owner,
and the blended one shapes its response **and** carries a degraded-catalog signal the picker shows the cook.

⛔ So deleting the proxy drops **three** things, each of which needs a named home before S6: the per-user
rate cap, the owner scoping, and the degraded-catalog signal. ⚠️ Food-service is not throttler-free either —
it runs an aggregate limiter. What it lacks is a **per-user** cap.

### 6.5 The test tiers misreport

Integration is invoked by many CI steps; e2e by four, ⛔ none a backend service. Recipe-service, identity, identity-webhooks and
food-service each ship an e2e script **no CI step calls** — ⚠️ four, not three; an earlier count missed one,
which is why C2 derives the list rather than quoting it. Separately, the integration-named files that reach a real database (119 of 217 on 2026-09-30, the count
X1's guard derives) are e2e tests under the current ruling.

### 6.6 Display names — four measured attempts, all of them failures

⛔ **HISTORICAL — THIS SECTION RECORDS WHY `food_variant` EXISTS. IT IS NOT A PROHIBITION.**
⚠️ It once read "nothing ships a better display name than a shortened USDA description"; §4.7 R53 now
takes the display name from **FoodOn**, on the owner ruling of 2026-09-22. All four measurements below
stand and none is disputed — what changed is the ARCHITECTURE they were measured against. Failures 1–3
remain live prohibitions. **Failure 4 is OVERTURNED**, and is the reason the variant table exists. Recorded here because the negative results are the load-bearing part: each
one is a design that looks obviously correct until it is measured, and the fourth — USDA's own FoodOn
names — looked so obviously correct that an earlier draft of §4.7 shipped it as a requirement before the
gates were run against it.

**What USDA actually ships.** USDA cross-references its foods to the **FoodOn** ontology and publishes
the mapping in `food_attribute.csv`. Verified three ways — raw CSV rows, reconciled row counts across
three `name` variants (`FoodOn Ontology Name #1/#2/For FDC Item`), and a 5-of-5 value match against the
live `POST /v1/foods` API for the same `fdc_id`s.

| dataset             | foods | FoodOn name | median | note                      |
| ------------------- | ----: | ----------: | -----: | ------------------------- |
| `sr_legacy_food`    | 7,793 | 4,126 (53%) |  19 ch | 49% of names are distinct |
| `foundation_food`   |   469 |   365 (78%) |  17 ch | 91% distinct              |
| `survey_fndds_food` | 5,432 |      0 (0%) |      — | none                      |
| `branded_food`      | 2.0 M |      0 (0%) |      — | none                      |

USDA's own `description` runs a median of **51** characters across ALL SR Legacy (**56** across the
covered subset R53 quotes — two populations, both measured, neither wrong), so a FoodOn name is far SHORTER — which is
what made it look like the answer. It is also free, licensed **CC0**, and needs no refresh of its own.
⛔ Shortness is not the requirement; ⚠️ failure 4 below is what shortness costs.

⛔ **COVERAGE RUNS BACKWARDS TO USAGE.** Joining SR Legacy to real
consumption (`input_food.sr_code` → `sr_legacy_food.NDB_number`) splits the catalog by whether anyone
actually eats the food:

| population                 | has FoodOn | no FoodOn | covered |
| -------------------------- | ---------: | --------: | ------: |
| eaten (an input to a dish) |        758 |       916 |     45% |
| never eaten                |      3,368 |     2,751 |     55% |

FoodOn is excellent on the beef and ham cross-products — the class that motivated this work — and
**absent on bread, cheddar, soy sauce, hamburger rolls, mayonnaise and mozzarella**. The 916 uncovered-
but-eaten foods run a median of **42** characters with only **15%** fitting 24, so they are both the
worst names and the ones a cook meets daily.

⛔ **FOUR CANDIDATES, ALL MEASURED, ALL FAILED.** Do not re-propose these:

1. **Four-way qualifier classifier** (identity / state / prep / lab-protocol over 17,713 segments) —
   median 51 → 43 ch, 15% fit 24 ch.
2. **USDA `Common Name` attribute (type 1000) as the display name** — 1,070 SR Legacy foods, and **84%
   of the values collide**: `hamburger` on 42 foods, `kfc` on 22, `family style` on 21,
   `longissimus dorsi` on 18. ⚠️ It remains a good **alias** source; it is not a name source, and
   §4.7 R53 takes the food's NAME from FoodOn rather than from any attribute value.
3. **Facet-stripping** (drop grade / trim / lab-protocol / commercial segments, recombine in natural
   order) — run over the 916: median 42 → 35 ch, 24-char fit 15% → 26%, 99% unique. ⛔ Rejected on
   QUALITY, not length: it emits names **longer than the original**
   (`Alcoholic beverage, distilled, all (gin, rum, vodka, whiskey) 80 proof` →
   `distilled all (gin rum vodka whiskey) 80 proof Alcoholic beverage`), wrong word order
   (`Salad dressing, mayonnaise, regular` → `mayonnaise Salad dressing`), and cannot touch a name with
   no comma (`Soy sauce made from soy and wheat (shoyu)` is returned unchanged). ⚠️ The root cause is
   general: whether a segment is identity or noise depends on the FOOD, not the string —
   `low moisture, part-skim` is noise on mozzarella while `dry mix, instant` is identity on pudding.

⛔ **FAILURE 4 IS OVERTURNED BY THE OWNER'S RULING OF 2026-09-22 — READ THIS BEFORE THE ENTRY BELOW.**
The MEASUREMENT stands and is not disputed. What it measured was a **FLAT** catalog, in which one
FoodOn name had to serve as the whole display name for every food mapped to it. §4.7's architecture
removes that premise: the FoodOn name is the **root**, and the qualifiers it drops become
**`food_variant`** rows one level down, where they stay visible and selectable. A name shared by 90
hams is a defect when it is the only name and correct when the 90 are reachable beneath it. ⚠️ The
entry is KEPT, not deleted, because its numbers are the reason the variant table must exist at all.

4. **USDA's FoodOn name as the display name** — _superseded, see above._ — the candidate §4.7 wrongly shipped, rejected on rev 4's
   OWN two gates. Run over all 4,126 SR Legacy foods that carry one:

    | gate                                                         |             result |
    | ------------------------------------------------------------ | -----------------: |
    | the FoodOn name is unique in the catalog                     |    1,317 (**32%**) |
    | foods whose USDA name carries a PROTECTED qualifier          |              1,786 |
    | ⛔ … of those, the FoodOn name DROPS the protected qualifier |    1,701 (**95%**) |
    | passes BOTH gates                                            | **1,164** of 4,126 |

    ⛔ So the mechanism fails hardest exactly where it matters. `Potatoes, hash brown, refrigerated,
unprepared` and `Potatoes, hash brown, refrigerated, prepared, pan-fried` **both** become
    `potato hash brown` — two different foods, one name, and `prepared` moves energy **44.5%**.
    `Pork, fresh, leg (ham), rump half, separable lean and fat, raw` and its `cooked` sibling both become
    `pork leg sirloin half`, losing the trim statement (20.3%) AND the cook state.

    ⛔ And it is WORSE than a shortened description rather than merely equal, for a structural reason:
    rev 4 can **redirect** truncation to preserve a protected segment, because it drops from a string that
    still contains it. A FoodOn name never contained the segment, so there is nothing to redirect to and no
    gate can repair it. ⚠️ The uniqueness predicate cannot detect the loss either — it compares finished
    strings and has no view of the energy the dropped words carried.

    ⚠️ The 1,164 foods that DO pass both gates are a real population, and using them would mean two
    different derivations of one column chosen per row. That is deliberately NOT proposed, and §4.7's
    architecture removes the need: the FoodOn name is the ROOT for all 4,126, and the qualifiers a gate
    would have adjudicated become `food_variant` rows one level down.

    ⛔ **THE PROTECTED-SEGMENT FIGURES ABOVE DO NOT REPRODUCE, AND THE COMMITTED SCRIPT IS NOW THE
    AUTHORITY.** `docs/reports/2026-09-21/displayLabelComposition.py` re-derives the protected class from
    rev 4's stated rule and reports **3,645** foods carrying a protected segment with the FoodOn name
    dropping it on **3,061 (84%)** — against the 1,786 / 1,701 (95%) recorded here, which came from an
    inline run whose script did not survive. The FoodOn join reproduces EXACTLY (4,126) and the
    uniqueness gate to within rounding (1,315 vs 1,317), so the divergence is the protected-token
    derivation and nothing else. ⚠️ The CONCLUSION is unaffected — a high rate either way — but until one
    derivation is settled, **quote neither pair as final**, and quote nothing whose script is not
    committed. This is the failure rev 4 records of rev 3, repeated inside one day.

⚠️ **What closes the remaining gap.** It is **enumerable and rankable by real usage** — 916 rows with a
consumption count each. That is a curation list, not an algorithm, and §4.3's correction path is already
the mechanism. ⛔ Two honest limits on the word "bounded". **916 is a FLOOR, not a total**: it counts only
SR Legacy foods that appear as an input to a WWEIA dish, so a food nobody in that survey ate and a cook
still types is not in it. And the bound is **scoped to today's roster** — `foundation` + `srLegacy`.
Enabling `surveyFndds` adds **5,432** foods with **zero** FoodOn coverage, which is six times the current
list, so that roster flip is also a curation-cost decision and not only a data-quality one. ⛔ A sampling caveat recorded so it is not repeated: an early 400-food sample put
FoodOn uniqueness at 92%; the full population is **49%**. Collisions rarely co-occur in a sample of that
size, so uniqueness MUST be measured over the whole population.

⚠️ **The food sync pipeline is NOT made redundant by this** — checked, because it looks like it should
be. `foodSources.dao.ts`'s change-refresh scan already carries `AND f.origin <> 'bulk'`, so it never
re-checked the frozen SR Legacy rows. Its remaining scope is exactly what the bulk download excludes:
the ~2 M Branded foods and live cache misses.

## 8. Data model

```
recipes 1 ──< 1..* ingredients ──1──> food_lookups ──┬── food          (BOUND: shared, one per food)
                (the row)                             ├── food variant (BOUND: shared, one per variant)
                                                      └── unresolved   (1:1 with the lookup)
```

⚠️ **Many rows may share one lookup on EITHER arm**: bound lookups dedup per food, and failure
records dedup per phrase — except a cook's declared name, which never converges.

**The row** carries: recipe, the lookup reference (required), amount, amount-high, unit, prep note,
section, sort order, the cook's own nutrition overrides, and — for imported recipes only — the raw
source line and the parsed phrase. ⛔ **No name column. No typed-in flag.**

**The lookup** carries: the food reference, the variant reference, the unresolved reference, the food's owner
(when private, root arm only), and a CHECK enforcing exactly one arm. The consumption prior did not move here
(migration 0051 records why). The food and variant references each have a **unique** partial index — one
lookup per food, one per variant — which is what makes KTD-6 necessary.

**The unresolved record** carries: the name that failed, the reason code, the status, the pending food
handle, which tiers were consulted and unavailable, bounded diagnostic codes, an attempt count and
timestamps, and a **normalized key**. ⛔ That key carries a unique index EXCEPT where the reason is "cook declared the name" — real failures converge, declarations never do. ⛔ No user column.

### 8.1 Where each row state comes from

⚠️ **A binding to a `food_variant` is its OWN state, not a flavour of "matched"** (§4.7 R59). Root-bound
means the cook chose no kind and a better default MAY later apply; variant-bound means they chose, and it
MUST NEVER be changed by a reseed. The two must not collapse into one row below.

⚠️ Added because the first version of this plan never said. Three of these are **not** in the new tables
and need no schema change.

| State                       | Source                                                               |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Matched                     | bound arm, food readable                                             |
| Matching / awaiting source  | unresolved arm, status + reason                                      |
| Unmatched (each cause)      | unresolved arm, reason code                                          |
| Cook typed it               | unresolved arm + "cook declared" reason (derived, never stored)      |
| Someone else's private food | bound arm + owner ≠ viewer, composed **last**                        |
| Food removed                | bound arm, food-service answers a definite _gone_                    |
| Error / offline             | bound arm, food-service unreachable — ⛔ distinct from _gone_        |
| **Needs review**            | ⛔ the **verification gate**, computed per row at read, never stored |
| **Ambiguous**               | ⛔ the **verification gate**, computed per row at read, never stored |

⛔ **Ambiguous keeps its "one pick binds every matching row" behaviour.** It works through the
author-scoped corrections, so a cook only ever affects their own rows — which is the shape KTD-5 made
safe.

## 16. Deferred to implementation

**The curated plan replaces items 1 and 3 of the first list below and amends item 4. See the banner at the top.**

⛔ **FOUR §4.7 BLOCKERS ARE OWNER DECISIONS, NOT IMPLEMENTATION DETAIL.** They gated U10, which the curated plan replaces, so they are kept here as the record.

✅ **OWNER DECISION (2026-09-22): §4.7 STAYS IN THIS PLAN.** It is not split into its own change.

1. **Where variant nutrition lives.** ✅ **RESOLVED BY OWNER RULINGS (2026-09-22) AND A DATABASE-ARCHITECT DESIGN.**
   The owner ruled: `food` keeps its name, its ids and its role as the root, and is NOT renamed; every food is a
   root; a root whose source item has no FoodOn name gets **no** `food_variant` row; a nullable **plain** foreign
   key is acceptable, but a nullable column inside a self-referencing table is not; and no data is duplicated
   between a root and its variants. The design that satisfies all of these:

    ```sql
    food          + default_variant_id text NULL        -- plain FK; NULL means "this root has no kinds"
                  + has_variants boolean GENERATED (default_variant_id IS NOT NULL) STORED NOT NULL
                  + UNIQUE (id, has_variants)
    food_variant  (id, food_id NOT NULL -> food, label NOT NULL,
                   in_group boolean GENERATED (true) STORED NOT NULL, UNIQUE (food_id, id),
                   FK (food_id, in_group) -> food (id, has_variants))   -- a kind exists only under a root with a default
    food          FK (id, default_variant_id) -> food_variant (food_id, id)   -- the default is one of THIS root's kinds
    each of the six per-item tables (sources, nutrients, portions, field provenance, category, popularity):
                  food_id NOT NULL (unchanged) + variant_id text NULL
                  + grouped boolean GENERATED (variant_id IS NOT NULL) STORED NOT NULL
                  FK (food_id, variant_id) -> food_variant (food_id, id)
                  FK (food_id, grouped)    -> food (id, has_variants)       -- own rows only on a root with no kinds
                  UNIQUE NULLS NOT DISTINCT (food_id, variant_id, <item key>)
    ```

    A root with no kinds holds its own rows (`variant_id` NULL). In a group, **every** source item is a kind,
    including the default, so the default's numbers sit on its own kind's rows. "The cook chose the default kind"
    binds the kind's id and "we defaulted" binds the root's id, so the two stay distinguishable. Changing the default
    is one column update and moves no data. Every rule is declarative; no trigger is needed. ⚠️ **Verified** on
    Postgres 18 in rolled-back transactions: a kind under a root with no default, a default naming another root's
    kind, an own-item row on a grouped root, a duplicate nutrient for one kind, a duplicate nutrient for an ungrouped
    root (`NULLS NOT DISTINCT`), and deleting the default kind are all refused; creating a grouped root with its kinds
    in one deferred unit of work and changing the default are accepted.

    **Migration.** ⛔ **THERE IS NO LIVE DATA, SO THERE IS NO DATA MIGRATION.** The schema above is written as its
    final shape in ONE migration, the same greenfield posture as `0051` and §12. There is no expand-then-contract
    sequence, no `NOT VALID` foreign keys, no old unique keys kept beside new ones, and no rule about preserving old ids.
    Field provenance, popularity and category assignment get surrogate primary keys from the start, because a primary
    key cannot include the nullable `variant_id`. The bulk seed writes roots and kinds directly: it groups source items
    by FoodOn term, mints root and kind ids with `ulidx` in TypeScript, creates each grouped root and its kinds in one
    deferred unit of work, and writes a root with no kinds, holding its own rows, for a source item with no FoodOn name.
    Rollback is a reseed into an empty database.

    ⚠️ **Read rule.** Batch reads must use two explicit branches (`variant_id IS NULL` for roots with no kinds,
    `variant_id = <default>` for grouped roots). `variant_id IS NOT DISTINCT FROM default_variant_id` is not usable as an
    index condition and scans every kind's rows; `ham (cured)` has 90.

    ⚠️ **Repo firsts.** These are the first `DEFERRABLE` constraints in the repository. Drizzle cannot declare them,
    so a test must pin `pg_constraint.condeferrable`. Drizzle support for `nullsNotDistinct()` and for foreign keys on
    generated columns is unverified.

    **Still open for the owner:** (a) ✅ R55 amended (see R55). (b) ✅ R59 decided: three arms (see R7 and R59).

2. **The grain.** ✅ **OWNER DECISION (2026-09-22): BARE ROOTS.** The owner asked whether splitting on calorie spread yields human-friendly names, which is the purpose of the FoodOn work. MEASURED (`docs/reports/2026-09-22/splitNameQuality.py`): **it does not.** The split adds source qualifiers back into the name. The median name grows from 19 to **26** characters, the p90 is **49**, and **21%** exceed 40 characters. The words repeat and read like laboratory labels: `ham (cured), cured, separable lean only`, `beef (ground), beef, meat`. ⛔ So splitting defeats the goal it was meant to protect. The recommendation is **bare roots** (the owner's original ruling), with the calorie difference carried by the kind picker and R37b2's disclosure, not by the name. The cost stays as measured: 11.9% of eating occasions need disclosure, and the worst root has 90 kinds, so `docs/design/ingredientSpecialization.md` must be re-specified for n=90.
3. **The election rule.** ✅ **REVISED BY OWNER DECISION (2026-09-22): the default is chosen ONLY AMONG AS-BOUGHT DETAILS** (raw, dry, uncooked, unprepared), because a recipe lists ingredients as bought and USDA computes recipes from uncooked ingredients, while the survey's most-eaten detail is usually cooked: the most-eaten default for `pasta` is cooked pasta (158 kcal/100 g), so "8 oz pasta" would count at about 43% of dry pasta's 371. Order: the plain member if it is as-bought; else the most-eaten as-bought detail; else the as-bought detail with the middle calorie value; only a food with no as-bought detail falls back to all its details. Search ranks as-bought foods first, which also covers the 426 foods that have a separate raw twin (`beef (ground)` beside `beef (ground, raw)`). Each detail is marked bought or eaten, automatically from the source words where they say it and by a person for the rest (138 foods with mixed details plus the 426 twin pairs, per `docs/design/ingredientSpecialization.md`). ⚠️ The error figures below measure the SUPERSEDED most-eaten rule against eating occasions, with R55's strip before plurals were folded (15 plain-member groups, now 23), and must be re-measured for the as-bought rule before they are quoted again. ✅ **OWNER DECISION (2026-09-22): most eaten, else the middle calorie value.** A grouped root with a plain member defaults to it (R55). Every other grouped root (703) defaults to the kind with the highest WWEIA consumption weight, already stored as `food_popularity`; where no member has consumption data (463 of 703), it defaults to the kind with the median energy, ties broken by lowest source id. MEASURED (`docs/reports/2026-09-22/electionRule.py`), as the calorie error weighted by what people actually eat: lowest id 35.0%, median energy 28.7%, most eaten 1.9%. ✅ **OWNER DECISION (2026-09-22): the plain member stays first, with no exceptions**, including vanilla, where people mostly eat the 56 kcal imitation but the plain member is the 288 kcal extract (about 10 kcal per teaspoon in a recipe). Of the 15 plain-member groups, 7 agree with "most eaten", 5 have no consumption data and 3 differ; two of the three differences are odd USDA groupings (orange juice concentrate under `orange peel (raw)`, baby food under `prune puree`), which go on the curation list. ⚠️ The default can change on a reseed when consumption data changes. A line bound to the root then shows the new default's numbers, which is the accepted meaning of "defaulted"; a line bound to a kind never moves.
4. **Kind identity across data updates.** ✅ **OWNER DECISION (2026-09-22).** A kind's id is keyed by its source item's own id (the crosswalk `(source, external_key)`, e.g. the USDA `fdc_id`), never by its label, so every reseed finds the existing kind and reuses its id, and a chosen kind never moves. ⛔ Changes in FoodOn or a source dataset reach the catalog ONLY through a **planned data update**: FoodOn is vendored static content (R60), so nothing changes at run time. The update procedure owns the regrouping rules. A root that gains a FoodOn name and must join an existing group keeps its id as a **kind id** in that group, so recipes keep the numbers the cook saw; a root that simply gains a name is renamed in place. ⛔ Before applying, the update produces a row-level diff that lists every root renamed, grouped or split and the recipe lines bound to each, and a person reviews it. The procedure is written as a runbook beside U10, not left to the operator's judgement.

## 17. Consequences the owner should see, not discover

1. ⚠️ **Reuse pays off from the third cook**, not the second. A's rename is personal; B's independent
   match makes it shared; C gets it free.
2. ✅ **A food landing in the catalog frees every waiting row at once**, because failures converge.
   ⚠️ Rows whose reason is "the cook declared the name" do not converge and are settled individually —
   correct, since two cooks writing the same words do not mean the same substance.
3. ⚠️ **A cook's own typed-in names are no longer searchable or de-duplicated**, and under the food-first
   filter that loss is felt in discovery too.
4. ⛔ **MOST ROWS WRAP AT THE NARROWEST WIDTH, AND THIS LINE USED TO SAY THE OPPOSITE.** "Roughly 1 in
   18" described rev 3's gate-OFF method; under rev 4's recommended layout the share of real ingredient
   occurrences that hold ONE line is far lower at 320 px and only reaches near-everything at 768 px, and
   at 200% text (WCAG 1.4.4) nothing holds one line at 320 px at all. The figures live in
   `docs/design/ingredientRowRev4.html` §7 and this plan quotes none of them on purpose — a restated
   number is exactly what rotted here. ⚠️ So wrapping is the NORMAL case on a phone, not the exception,
   and the row must be designed for two lines rather than treating the second as a rare fallback.
5. ⚠️ **The shortened label is catalog-global — and reaches only the ~43% of roots with no FoodOn term (R33)** — adding one food can lengthen another's label, so it is
   recomputed per refresh batch, not per row.
6. ⚠️ **Food-service becomes publicly browser-reachable.** That is the largest security change here.
7. ⚠️ **016 and 017 wait on this.** ⛔ No velocity estimate is offered — that is the owner's to set.
