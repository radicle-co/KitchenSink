# Should Free plans get AI? Research for Commise

Prepared 2026-10-27. Read-only research. Evidence is labelled: [P] primary source (company page or filing), [S] secondary (blog, news, aggregator), [J] my judgement. Where a number came only from [S] sources that disagreed, it says "unconfirmed".

## 1. Bottom line

- The market answer is not "free AI or no free AI". Almost every company that keeps a free plan gives a small taste of AI and puts volume, the best model, and multi-step or "agent" work behind the paid plan.
- The one-use-is-one-call cost is small (your model: $450-$1,500 a month at 100k users). What costs money is multi-step work. Gate on cost shape, not on the feature name.
- No published study I found proves that free AI raises or lowers conversion. The best-quality number is a RevenueCat sample on AI apps in general, and it is not about free tiers. Be honest about that with the owner.
- Recommendation: Option B below (a small free taste of single-call AI, a time-limited full taste on Pro with AI via a reverse-trial style offer, planning and optimization paid only). BYOK: paid plans only, not on Free, and not as a way to skip the allowance on Pro with AI. See sections 5 and 6.

Patterns across the table [J]:

1. Free plans almost always get a small, capped taste (Figma, Canva, Grammarly, Miro, Adobe). The cap is a monthly pool, not a feature wall. Notion is the main trial-only case.
2. The paid line is volume plus the heavy or multi-step features (Atlassian charges more credits for agents; Adobe and Figma sell extra credit packs).
3. Direction of travel is toward metering. Figma moved to enforced credits in 2026; Atlassian turns on metered overage in December 2026. Bundling happened at Notion and Microsoft (price rise), with Microsoft keeping a no-AI plan as an exit.
4. Vendors are not shy about changing their mind. Duolingo is moving AI features down to a cheaper tier as cost per call falls.

## 3. Research and analysis (section 2 of the brief)

Strength labels: A = large sample, primary; B = credible analyst or vendor data, method partly shown; C = opinion or unsourced.

1. AI apps vs non-AI apps, RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (strength A on what it measures, but it does not compare free-AI vs paid-only designs). Via TechCrunch: median trial-to-paid 8.5% for AI apps vs 5.6% for non-AI; realised revenue per payer higher (annual plan $30.16 vs $21.37); annual retention 21.1% vs 30.7%; annual plans cancelled about 30% faster. Sample: RevenueCat's customer base, "AI-powered" apps are 27.1%; "AI" includes apps that market themselves as AI-powered. https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows . Meaning [J]: AI wins the first purchase and loses the renewal. So a design that gives AI to Free users and lets them pay for it and churn is not obviously good. The novelty fades.
2. Free-to-paid benchmarks, ChartMogul 2026 conversion report via secondary summaries (strength B): median freemium free-to-paid is 8% across products, with a quarter below 2.5%, and over 15% rare. I could not read the report itself. https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/ (found by search; see a summary at https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/blog/state-of-freemium-2026-conversion-rates-revenue-share-failure-modes). Claims that AI freemium converts 15-20% are unsourced (C).
3. Reverse trials, Elena Verna at Amplitude (strength C): says freemium typically converts about 5% with about 25% staying active, and that a reverse trial "may" get about 15%. She names no companies and no sample; it is a projection. https://amplitude.com/blog/reverse-trial . Secondary pages quote "10-40% lift" and "38% higher conversion"; I could not trace either, so I do not use them.
4. Cost of serving (strength B/C): Modelled example from SaaStr: once heavy users are more than 10-15% of a base, a flat per-seat AI price stops working. This is a model, not company data. Reported negative gross margins for AI coding tools (Cursor, Replit) are secondhand estimates. https://www.saastr.com/cursor-our-users-love-per-seat-pricing-its-just-the-cost-side-makes-it-harder ; https://www.saastr.com/have-ai-gross-margins-really-turned-the-corner-the-real-math-behind-openais-70-compute-margin/ . Relevance to Commise [J]: the risk is the heavy user on a flat plan, not the free taster. Your own model agrees (a whole weekly plan costs about 8x one call).
5. Willingness to pay for AI (strength B, wrong question for you): 2025 surveys say few people will pay extra for AI inside a product (CNET/YouGov via eMarketer: half of US smartphone owners would not pay extra; ZDNET/Aberdeen: about 8%). https://www.emarketer.com/content/consumers-unwilling-pay-ai-features-1 ; https://about.getcoai.com/news/most-americans-arent-that-into-ai-but-they-do-like-it-for-photo-editing . A 2026 Rev survey says 60% would pay something to keep access if free tools vanished. https://www.rev.com/blog/how-much-is-ai-worth . None measures how people feel about an AI paywall. I found no study on that. Treat "users hate AI paywalls" and "users will pay" as both unproven.
6. Duolingo as a live case (strength B, secondary): management moved a premium AI feature toward the cheaper tier as inference cost fell. Say so to the owner: the right free/paid line moves over time. https://www.tikr.com/blog/duolingo-cut-its-priciest-ai-feature-to-under-a-penny-a-call-heres-what-that-does-to-margins .
7. BYOK in consumer apps: no uptake or support-load numbers found anywhere (gap). Who offers it: mostly developer and power-user tools (Raycast, Warp, GitHub Copilot app). https://manual.raycast.com/ai/bring-your-own-key ; https://docs.warp.dev/agents/inference/bring-your-own-api-key.md . Findings: a chat subscription (Claude Pro/Max, ChatGPT Plus) cannot be used as the key; Anthropic updated its terms in Feb 2026 to bar subscription OAuth tokens in third-party tools [S] https://winbuzzer.com/2026/02/19/anthropic-bans-claude-subscription-oauth-in-third-party-apps-xcxwbn/ . So the cook needs a paid developer API account, which most cooks do not have. Raycast warns users to watch provider billing for surprise charges (support signal). Warp keeps keys on the device; a server-stored personal key was flagged as a concern by OpenAI support in a forum thread (https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/terms-of-service-question-about-a-bring-your-own-api-key-byok-saas-feature/178632, strength C).

## 4. Legal and store guardrails (section 3 of the brief)

- EU DSA Article 25: bars online platforms from designing interfaces that deceive, manipulate or materially distort free and informed choice. Commission can issue guidance (nagging, hard-to-cancel, false hierarchy). I could not fetch the official text; summary from https://presencis.com/regulations/dsa/article-25/ and https://osborneclarke.com/insights/eus-digital-service-act-introduce-first-express-ban-dark-patterns . [J] Article 25 binds "online platforms" (public sharing of user content), and practices already covered by consumer-protection or GDPR rules are carved out. Commise's public recipes may make it a platform; either way, treat the rule as binding for design.
- EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 on deceptive design patterns: final version adopted 2023-02-14; six categories (Overloading, Skipping, Stirring, Obstructing, Fickle, Left in the Dark). https://www.edpb.europa.eu/node/3084_en . Scope is social media, but the categories are a usable checklist. Relevant to credits: counters that nag, countdowns, a cancel path harder than the sign-up path.
- FTC "click to cancel": the 2024 rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 2025-07-08 on procedure. The FTC restarted rulemaking with an ANPRM published 2026-03-13, comments due 2026-04-13. ROSCA and FTC Act s.5 still apply. https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule ; https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-19-ftc-issues-new-advance-notice-of-proposed-rulemaking-on-negative-option-marketing . Practical: a trial that turns into a paid plan must show price, date, and an equal-effort cancel path. The reverse-trial option below falls under this.
- Apple App Store guideline 3.1.1: credits bought by in-app purchase may not expire. Subscriptions may include consumable credits (3.1.2(a)), so a monthly allowance that resets inside a subscription is fine. Verified on https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ . Practical: do not sell stand-alone "AI top-up" credits with an expiry. If top-ups are ever sold, they cannot expire on iOS. 5.1.2(i): you must disclose and get explicit permission before sharing personal data with third-party AI. That binds sending a cook's recipes or key to a model vendor and any outside agent.
- Google Play: virtual currency can only be spent in the app it was bought in; digital items and subscriptions must use Play Billing. Expiry rule for credits not confirmed. https://play.google.com/about/monetization-ads/payments ; AI-generated content policy: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294 .
- Credit meters and the spec's own rules [J]: show the count honestly (used / left / resets on), do not make the paid prompt louder as credits run low, do not hide the free tier's value, and make Free's last-use screen a plain statement rather than a countdown.

## 5. Packaging options for Commise

Facts that drive this [from aiCostModel.md]: free credits of 3 / 5 / 10 uses a month cost about $450 / $750 / $1,500 a month at 100k users when one use is one call; a weekly auto-plan costs about 8x. Recommended Pro with AI allowance in the model: 50 uses (a weekly auto-plan counts one per recipe it writes). Those are your own guesses of usage, not measurements.

### Option A: No free AI; BYOK on paid only

- For: zero AI cost on Free; the cleanest upsell story; Spotify does exactly this [S].
- Against: it is the rarest choice among the apps in section 2, where nearly every product with a free plan gives a small capped taste. Peers sell AI as the paid upgrade but let people try it first. With no taste, the first sight of AI is a price of $19.99, double Pro. RevenueCat says AI apps convert well on trial, which means the trial is the place to show it, but a trial needs a card or a sign-up wall. Free users never learn what the feature does.
- Evidence strength: weak either way; no study compares.

### Option B (recommended): small free taste of single-call AI; multi-step and optimization paid; a time-limited full taste

- Free: 3 uses a month of single-call features only (generate one recipe, suggest a few). Counter always visible. Over the limit: plain "you used 3 of 3, resets on the 1st" with one upgrade link.
- Pro (no AI): same 3 as Free. No bigger. This keeps $9.99 clearly a "no AI" plan and avoids a plan that is worse than free.
- Pro with AI: 50-use allowance; auto-plan, optimization, food-waste planning and outside agents live here only.
- Taste of the paid features: the 14-day trial of Pro with AI runs with a trial allowance (your model assumed 10 uses). That is a reverse-trial-like shape for the AI tier only [J].
- For: matches the Figma, Grammarly, Canva, Miro, Adobe pattern (small free pool, bigger paid pool). Cost is small and known ($450 a month at 3 uses). Lets the free cook feel the value of "I typed what I have and got a dinner", the thing the upsell sells. Gate on cost shape: the 8x-cost feature is paid only, which protects margin.
- Against: it spends $450+ a month for conversion that nobody has measured. Risk the free taste is enough for casual cooks and lowers upgrades. RevenueCat says AI payers churn faster, so extra payers from the taste may be low-quality. Needs a counter and an upgrade screen on both platforms.
- Mitigation: build the meter and log "free AI use then upgrade" events from day one so you can cut to 0 or raise to 5 on data. Review at 90 days.

### Option C: AI free only during the trial (reverse trial), nothing after

- Everyone gets a Pro with AI trial at sign-up (or on first AI tap) for 14 days; on expiry they drop to Free with no AI.
- For: cleanest cost cap; Verna argues it combines trial conversion with a kept free base (projection, not data) [C]. Notion is nearest: full-ish AI as a trial on Free [P].
- Against: a cook who tries AI in week one and is cooking again in week six sees the feature vanish. Resentment risk from taking it away (the Microsoft 365 price rise drew complaints [S]; I did not find a study of take-away reactions). Needs trial-to-paid disclosure rules (FTC/ROSCA above); a card-less trial avoids most of that. Recipe use is bursty and seasonal, so 14 days may never touch the planning feature.

Not recommended: a large free pool (10+), because cost scales with usage and the whole plan becomes a taste; and sold credit top-ups at launch, because of Apple's no-expiry rule and added complexity.

## 6. BYOK recommendation by plan

- Free: no. A free plan with BYOK is unlimited AI for $0 to us, which kills the upsell, and the cook still cannot use ChatGPT or Claude subscriptions as a key. Most free cooks will not have an API account (uptake data is a gap, so this is judgement).
- Pro ($9.99, no AI): yes, as the way into AI for the cook who already has a key. It fits the plan's identity (you pay us for the app, you pay your vendor for AI), costs us nothing, and does not cannibalize the $19.99 plan much because the buyer is a technical minority [J].
- Pro with AI: yes, but not as a way to skip the allowance, and not as a requirement. Let them use their key after the allowance runs out, or for any provider they prefer.
- Hard requirements if built: store the key server-side encrypted or on-device only (decide once, state it in the privacy text); never log it; show the cook's own usage as the vendor's, not ours; warn about vendor billing (Raycast does); disclose that recipe text is sent to that vendor (Apple 5.1.2(i)); treat subscription OAuth as not supported. Outside agents (ChatGPT, Gemini) reading and writing recipes through OAuth should be paid-only for write access; read access could follow the plan rule. This part I did not research; it is my suggestion.
- Support burden: no published data. Plan for "my key stopped working" and "I got a surprise bill" tickets. Measure and revisit.

## 7. Gaps and what would change the answer

- No study compares conversion with free AI vs paid-only AI for a consumer app. The first 90 days of your own events are the evidence. Ship the meter and the events before the feature.
- Not confirmed against primary pages: Canva, Miro, Google, Duolingo (Max details), Samsung Food, Ollie prices, Microsoft credits today, Zapier (no data), Mealime closure, Cooklist (no data).
- Cost figures are the aiCostModel.md guesses (usage low confidence).
- If your own data later shows free tasters convert under about 1 point better than no-taste, drop to Option A. If they do and cost stays small, raise to 5.
