# Price evidence for the Commise Pro price choice

Question: Pro at $99.99/yr ($9.99/mo) or $59.99/yr (about $5.99/mo).
All pages were opened on 2026-10-27 unless a line says otherwise.
Labels: [P] primary source. [S] secondary source. [Unconfirmed] not checked against a primary page.
GAP means I looked and found no reliable number.
Text in quotation marks is copied exactly from the source, so it can break the plain-English style.

## Read this first

- The RevenueCat and Adapty price-tier numbers compare different apps at different prices. They are not price tests. High-priced apps may sit in different categories with different buyers. So they show a pattern, not a cause.
- RevenueCat does not define price tiers in dollars. A tier is relative to the other apps measured.
- The controlled tests below (Mojo, Adapty travel app, PropGPT, BuyBye, Lose It) are vendor or company stories. They are one app each, with no sample sizes or confidence intervals shown.
- No source covers recipe or Food and Drink apps by price tier.

Other lines from the same page:

- Market price level: "median yearly price increased to $34.80 (from $31.60), while weekly ($5.99) and monthly ($10) remained flat. The P90 yearly range expanded (up to $90)". So $99.99/yr sits above the RevenueCat P90 for yearly plans. $59.99/yr sits well above the median.
- Spread: "Yearly plans show widest spread (from $17–20 to $58–59)." The page does not say which percentiles these are.
- Where price hurts: "Price influences early churn, particularly on yearly plans, but whether you use freemium or a hard paywall does not meaningfully change retention."
- Survivors: "High-priced lose more users early but retain survivors equally, showing 3rd renewal parity."
- Why people cancel (Google Play churn survey): "'Cost Related' churn reasons are between 25-45% for all categories."
- Trial length: "trial-to-paid median is 25.5% (≤4 days), 37.4% (5–9 days), and 42.5% (17–32 days)."
- Trial-to-paid by price tier: GAP. The 2026 page shows no tier split for this metric.
