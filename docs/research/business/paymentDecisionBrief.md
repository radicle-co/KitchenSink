## Summary on one page

- **The choice is really two choices.** (a) On the phone apps: sell through Apple's and Google's in-app purchase, send people to the web to pay, or do both. (b) On the web: use a plain card processor (we are the seller) or a "merchant of record" (they are the seller). A third piece, a subscription service such as RevenueCat, joins the web and both stores into one record of who has paid.
- **Market data says most subscription money comes through the stores, not the web.** Web purchases are **3.2%** of revenue worldwide and **4.9% in North America** across 115,000+ apps on RevenueCat [P] ([RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps), 27 Oct 2026). iOS carries **85%** of app subscription revenue [P] ([Adapty State of In-App Subscriptions 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/), 27 Oct 2026; its data is mostly iOS apps).
- **So the store fee is the main cost, and at our size it is 15%.** Apple's Small Business Program charges 15% while proceeds stay at or under $1M a year [P] ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/), 27 Oct 2026). Google charges 10% plus a 5% billing fee on subscriptions in the US, EU, UK, Japan and Australia [P] ([Google Play service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en), 27 Oct 2026).
- **Which web provider we choose barely changes the total at the market mix.** At 100,000 registered users and 5% conversion, all on Pro, every "store in-app purchase + web provider" stack costs **$6,945–$7,010 a month (15.5–15.7% of revenue)** [D]. The merchant-of-record premium is **$1 to $132 a month** across the whole grid [D].
- **Sending US iPhone buyers to the web saves about 2.7 points of revenue only if as many people still buy.** The one clean test found fewer buyers: the in-app + web option kept **86¢ per in-app dollar** at a 15% store fee [P] ([RevenueCat Dipsea test](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/iap-vs-web-purchases-conversion-test), 27 Oct 2026). Our break-even is a **4.0%** loss of iPhone proceeds [D]. The test lost more than that, though its web price was discounted 30%.
- **The tax rule that matters most is not size.** An overseas seller must register for UK VAT from its first UK sale [P] ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration), 27 Oct 2026). For the EU, the €10,000 threshold applies only to sellers based in one EU state, so a US seller has none [Inference from [P] [EU OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en), 27 Oct 2026]. A merchant of record takes that duty away.
- **Recommendation (section 7):** in-app purchase on both stores everywhere; Stripe Billing on the web, connected through RevenueCat's Stripe integration; turn on Stripe Managed Payments (Stripe as seller) if Stripe approves us, otherwise limit web checkout to US buyers until a merchant of record is in place; build a US-only "pay on the web" link on iPhone behind a remote switch, keep it **off**, and turn it on only after Apple's US rate is set and our own test shows it does not lose more than about 4% of iPhone proceeds.

## 1. The decision

Commise needs one way to charge people for Pro and Pro with AI on the web, on iPhone and on Android, and to know in every app, at every moment, whether a person has paid. Two linked choices make that up.

**(a) Mobile: how people pay inside the phone apps.**

- **In-app purchase (IAP).** Apple or Google takes the payment and is the seller. Cost: 15% at our size.
- **Link-out to the web.** The app sends the person to our web checkout. Allowed with no Apple permission only on the US storefront, and Apple's US fee for it is not yet set. Google charges 10% on US link-out subscriptions.
- **Hybrid.** IAP stays (Apple requires it, see 3.2), plus a "pay on the web" link where allowed.

**(b) Web: who takes the money on commise.app.**

- **A processor** (for example Stripe). We are the seller. We own tax registration, refunds and disputes.
- **A merchant of record** (for example Stripe Managed Payments or Paddle). They are the seller. They own tax, refunds and disputes, for a higher fee.
- **The joining layer.** Whatever we pick, something must merge the App Store, Google Play and web purchases into one record of who has paid for what. RevenueCat is the main candidate; building it ourselves is the alternative.

The choices interact. If the phone apps use link-outs, more money flows through the web, so the web choice (processor or merchant of record) matters more.

## 2. Plain definitions

| Term                                      | What it means here                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Processor**                             | A company that charges the card and passes the money to us, for example Stripe. We are the legal seller. We must collect and file sales tax or VAT where required, answer refund requests and fight chargebacks.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Merchant of record (MoR)**              | A company that buys the subscription from us and resells it to the cook. The cook's receipt and card statement name them, not us. They collect and file tax, handle refunds and chargebacks, and take a larger fee. Apple and Google are the merchant of record for every in-app purchase.                                                                                                      |
| **Entitlement / one subscription record** | A single answer to "does this person have Pro, Pro with AI, or nothing, right now?", kept in one place no matter where they paid. Without it, a person who paid on the web would look unpaid on iPhone.                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Link-out**                              | A button in the phone app that opens our web checkout in the browser, so the cook pays us directly instead of through Apple or Google.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Small Business Program**                | Apple's 15% rate (instead of 30% in a subscriber's first year) for developers with up to $1M in proceeds the prior calendar year and up to $1M in the current year [P] ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/), 27 Oct 2026). "Proceeds" means sales after Apple's commission and certain taxes. Above $1M in a year, the standard rate applies to later sales. |
| **Sales tax / VAT nexus**                 | The point at which a government requires us to register, charge its tax and file returns. In US states it is usually set by sales into that state; in the UK and EU, for a seller based abroad, it starts at the first sale (section 5.2).                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Chargeback (dispute)**                  | The cardholder asks their bank to reverse a charge. The seller pays a fee and must send evidence.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

Two more facts that apply everywhere:

- A recipe app is not a "reader app". Apple's reader list is "magazines, newspapers, books, audio, music, and video" [Inference from [P]] ([Apple reader apps](https://developer.apple.com/support/reader-apps), read by the source research). So we cannot drop IAP.

**What the tables show [D]:**

1. **With IAP everywhere, the web provider changes the total by under 1% of revenue.** The store fee is ~95% of the cost. Choose the web provider for tax, risk and effort, not price.
2. **The merchant-of-record premium is tiny at the market mix:** Managed Payments costs $1–$65 a month more than plain Stripe with everyone on Pro, $3–$132 with everyone on Pro with AI. That is less than Stripe Tax Complete's $90/month floor, before any adviser's fees.
3. **The hybrid saves 2.7–3.0 points of revenue, but only if as many people buy (see 4.4).** At 100k / 5% on Pro that is $1,235 a month.
4. **If Apple sets its proposed fee, the hybrid saving shrinks or reverses.** At 100k / 5% on Pro: saving $1,235 at 0%, $584 at 5% (Small Business proposal), **−$717 at 15%** [D].
5. **RevenueCat is free in the smallest cells** and reaches $447–$894 a month at the top.
6. **Apple's $1M limit is not reached in this grid** (top cell $737k with everyone on Pro with AI). ## 7. Recommendation

**Build this, in this order:**

1. **In-app purchase on iPhone and Android, in every market** (option A). Enrol in Apple's Small Business Program first.
2. **Stripe Billing on the web, connected through RevenueCat's Stripe Billing integration.** Use the Clerk user ID as RevenueCat's app user ID on all three platforms.
3. **Ask Stripe for Managed Payments now.** If approved, tick "Use Managed Payments when available" from launch. If not, keep Stripe as self-seller and **accept only US billing addresses** until a merchant of record (Managed Payments or Paddle) is live.
4. **Build the US-only "pay on the web" link on iPhone behind a remote switch, and leave it off.** Turn it on for a test only when Apple's US rate is set, and keep it on only if our own test shows it loses less than about 4% of iPhone proceeds.
5. **Do not build link-outs for Android, the EU, Japan or elsewhere.** At 10% plus card fees they cost the same as IAP or more (4.6).
6. **Build the duties in section 5 into the paywall and account screens** whatever the payment choice.

**Why:**

- **The store fee is the cost that matters, and it is already at its lowest rate (15%).** At the market mix, about 95% of revenue goes through the stores [P], so the web provider changes total cost by under 1 point [D].
- **The merchant of record is cheap insurance at our mix.** It costs $1–$132 a month more across the grid [D], less than Stripe Tax Complete's $90 floor. In return it removes UK and EU registration from the first sale [P / Inference] and takes refunds and chargebacks off a tiny team.
- **Stripe through RevenueCat keeps the most doors open.** It is the only path where switching to Managed Payments is a checkbox with no extra RevenueCat fee [P], and our customers' cards stay in our own Stripe account until we tick it.
- **The evidence does not support link-outs at 15%.** The one clean test kept 86¢ per IAP dollar with a web option at a 15% fee [P]; our break-even is a 4.0% loss [D]. The US rate is also still open in two courts [S].
- **RevenueCat costs nothing until $2,500 a month** and 1% after [P]; building the same receipt, notification and entitlement handling ourselves has no published effort estimate, and every part of it is on-call work.

**What would change the recommendation:**

| If this happens                                                                 | Then                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stripe refuses Managed Payments                                                 | Use Paddle through RevenueCat (ask its $9.99 price first), or stay self-seller US-only                                                      |
| Commise's measured web share is far above 4.9% (say a third or more)            | Re-run section 4; the merchant-of-record premium grows (4.5) and RevenueCat Web Billing or self-seller Stripe gets more attractive on price |
| Apple's US link-out fee is set at 0–5% and our test shows little loss in buyers | Turn the hybrid link on in the US                                                                                                           |
| Apple's US fee is set at 15%, or the Supreme Court rules for Apple              | Leave the link off; consider removing it                                                                                                    |
| Apple proceeds approach $1M a year                                              | The 30% first-year rate makes web purchases worth more; reconsider the hybrid and web-first promotion of annual plans                       |
| Google's US fees are struck down                                                | Re-check Android link-out                                                                                                                   |
| EU, UK, Canada or Australia launch before a merchant of record is live          | Block those countries at web checkout, or register for VAT/GST first                                                                        |
| Lock-in on Managed Payments cards is judged unacceptable                        | Start self-seller US-only, and ask Stripe how to move subscriptions later                                                                   |

**Confidence:** High on fees and store rules (primary pages). Medium on the channel mix (market data from mobile-first apps, not apps with a full web product). Low on conversion effects (one controlled test, vendor-run, with a confounding discount) and on tax conclusions until an adviser confirms them.
