## 5. Email list rules

**CAN-SPAM (US).** The FTC lists eight rules: true header information, honest subject lines, a clear ad notice, a physical postal address, a clear opt-out, honoring opt-out within 10 business days, keeping the opt-out working for at least 30 days, and not shifting responsibility to a company that sends for you. Each violating email can cost up to $53,088. The postal address can be a street address, a post office box registered with the U.S. Postal Service, or a private mailbox registered with a commercial mail receiving agency established under Postal Service regulations (same FTC guide). Both the advertiser and the sender can be liable. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business (primary).

**GDPR (EU/UK).** Article 7: the sender must be able to prove the person consented. The consent request must be clearly separate, in plain language. People can withdraw at any time, and withdrawing must be as easy as giving consent. Consent is not "freely given" if a service is conditional on consent that is not necessary for it. Source: https://gdpr-info.eu/art-7-gdpr/ (reproduction of the legal text. the official text is on EUR-Lex). Note: I did not research whether a platform acts as controller or processor when it holds a creator's list. That needs a lawyer.

**CASL (Canada).** The government page says CASL covers commercial electronic messages and requires consent before sending them. The page I might read did not give the details on express versus implied consent, unsubscribe timing, or penalties. GAP for details. Source: https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en (primary). Read the full law at the Justice Laws link on that page before building.

## What this means

1. **Ads are the main income, and the platform must not cut it.** Ads were 42% of income in one survey. RPM averages $36.81 in a small 2025 food survey. Raptive keeps 25%, Journey keeps 30%. Rows: 1.1. A layer that shows its own ads or removes the creator's ads would replace the biggest income line.
2. **Entry bars are low and falling.** Journey needs 1,000 sessions and Raptive 25,000 pageviews (Oct 2025). A small creator can already earn ads. the layer cannot assume the creator has no ad partner. Rows: 1.1.
3. **Search traffic is a real, but not measured, risk.** Creators report 30-80% drops. Raptive's own network data shows most hit sites recovered, and one 2024 figure shows only 7%. So the pitch should be "less dependence on Google", not "Google is dead". Rows: section 2.
4. **Fee benchmark for native publishing.** Other platforms take 0% to 10% of subscription income before card fees: Beehiiv 0%, Ghost 0%, Substack 10%, Patreon 10%, Gumroad 10% + $0.50, Kit 3.5% + $0.30. YouTube gives 55% of ad revenue. Rows: section 3.
5. **App-store fees change the math for in-app sales.** Apple takes 15% under $1M or 30% standard. Google 10-15% on subscriptions plus a 5% billing fee in some regions. Patreon shows creators must raise iOS prices about 43% to cover Apple's 30%. Selling creator content inside the Commise app is expensive. web checkout is cheaper. Rows: section 3 (Apple, Google, Patreon).
6. **Lock-in is a known complaint.** Yummly closed with no bulk export, while Substack and Beehiiv market free list export. A free, full export of recipes and subscriber lists is a trust feature. Rows: Yummly (section 4), Substack export (section 3).
7. **Creators already use WordPress recipe plugins.** WP Recipe Maker has 50,000+ active installs. A layer that reads those recipe cards, rather than asking for retyping, fits where creators already work. Rows: section 4.
8. **No public pay terms exist for recipe-app creator programs.** Chefadora is the only one with posted terms (ad share, 10,000-view threshold, percent unknown). Commise would be setting the market, not copying it. Also, email obligations (opt-out in 10 business days, proof of consent, withdrawal as easy as signup) apply when the platform holds or sends a creator's list. Rows: section 4, section 5.
